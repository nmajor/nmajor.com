# Draft assignment

Write the first prose draft of a short news-shaped essay for Nicholas Major's newsletter,
Actual Intelligence. Output only the essay body in Markdown. Do not add frontmatter, a title,
notes, a source list, or commentary about your work.

The title is fixed: **The 16.4 million-hour search box**

The first sentence is fixed and must appear verbatim:

> Farmers Insurance is touring a 16.4 million-hour AI success story built around a support bot.

## Argument

Farmers is presenting a 16.4 million-hour AI success story, but the only concrete AI product
it has described is askfarmers.ai, an internal support-search assistant. Farmers has not shown
how much of the headline saving came from that bot. Five comparable deployments still show
that well-built internal search assistants can save measurable time and can plausibly have a
real ROI. A useful support bot does not need an industry-reinvention story to justify itself.

## Source material

Use only facts supported in the attached reporting brief and comparables report. Preserve all
attributions and limitations. Link the relevant primary or reporting source inline at the
claim it supports. Do not claim that the five positive cases establish how often these tools
succeed. Do not attribute Farmers' entire 16.4 million hours to askfarmers.ai.

## Voice and shape

- Write 500 to 700 words. Concise and punchy beats comprehensive.
- Sound like a skeptical senior engineer talking to another operator over coffee.
- Use first person only for Nicholas Major's actual judgment supplied here. Do not invent
  experience, clients, projects, conversations, or feelings.
- State what Farmers did, what the bot does, what is and is not measured, and what the five
  comparisons add.
- Spend the most space on Farmers. Compress the comparisons into a few carefully chosen
  examples rather than five repetitive mini case studies.
- End on a direct observation, not advice. Do not tell the reader what to do, ask a closing
  question, prescribe a lesson, or write "the takeaway is."
- The closing idea should be: a support bot can earn a real return without remaking an
  industry. Phrase it naturally and directly.
- No em dashes.
- No hashtags.
- No rule-of-three list used for rhythm.
- No "It's not X, it's Y" or "not just X, but Y" construction.
- Never use evaluative preambles such as "the useful part," "the interesting part," "the
  best part," or any "the [adjective] part" construction.
- Avoid writerly preambles such as "here's the thing," "what strikes me," and "what I keep
  coming back to."
- Do not use these hype words in the prose: revolutionize, transform, groundbreaking,
  game-changing, unlock, leverage, harness, seamless, cutting-edge.
- Vary paragraph and sentence length. Do not make every paragraph a one-line declaration.
- Do not repeat the title as a heading. Use at most two short sentence-case subheadings, and
  only if they genuinely help.
- Use straight quotation marks and apostrophes.

Nick's raw phrasing, for tone rather than verbatim repetition:

- "Farmers are making the rounds, talking about how they revolutionized their business with
  AI. All they did was build a freaking AI search assistant."
- "It's super underwhelming."
- "If it's an AI support search helper, if it's done right, these things can have an ROI."
