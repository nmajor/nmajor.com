Intercom charges 99 cents when its AI resolves a customer support ticket. It also charges 99 cents when the AI gives up and hands that ticket to a person.

There's some careful small print about which handovers count, but the effect is that they bill for plenty of tickets the AI never actually resolved.

Their competitors don't all work that way. Zendesk only bills for tickets closed without a human getting involved. Decagon and Sierra don't charge for escalated cases at all, and Decagon's own glossary admits that "defining what a resolution is can be tricky".

Here's the part I find genuinely useful, and it goes well beyond support software.

Watch what a company charges for rather than what it promises. Building a billing line around the handover is a bet that handovers stay common enough to be worth money. Nobody builds revenue on a problem they expect to solve.

The marketing says AI handles your customers. The pricing says budget for people to keep finishing the job. I'd trust the pricing.
