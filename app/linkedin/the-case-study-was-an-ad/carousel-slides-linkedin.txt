## LinkedIn document post: slide-by-slide copy

NOT A SCHEDULABLE POST. Slide copy for personal-carousel.md (.txt on purpose).
Upload as a 9-page PDF. LinkedIn shows a "9 pages" badge, which is why the
exact-count cover works especially well here.

**Framework:** A, Value-Stack (external skill, carousel-frameworks.md)
**Why this framework:** the content is a list of checks. Completeness is the
promise and the count is the hook. Platform note in the skill: "Value-Stack's
exact-count cover plays especially well" on LinkedIn document posts.
**Slides:** 9 (framework range 4-14). Cover + 7 checks + close. Exactly 7
delivered, no filler.
**Format:** square or 4:5. Same layout, type scale and palette on slides 2-8.

Every check below is one Nick actually ran in the essay, and each names the story
that failed it. No check is invented; no number appears here that is not in the piece.

---

### Slide 1: Cover
**7 checks before you believe an AI savings number**

Run these on the next case study someone forwards you.

---

### Slide 2: Check 1: Who published it, and what do they sell?
The most-quoted cautionary tale about building your own tools:

- Custom support-triage tool replaced a $490-a-month subscription
- Real six-month cost came out higher
- Every byline on the site belongs to staff at a low-code and AI development agency
- The footer on every page advertises that agency
- The About page says the site has "no vendor deals"

---

### Slide 3: Check 2: Does the post it cites actually exist?
That piece opens: "Six months ago, we documented what looked like a clean win."

- There is no such post
- The only earlier piece on the same product quotes completely different numbers
- Its URL now returns a 404
- Zero results for the domain on Hacker News, every tag, no date limit

---

### Slide 4: Check 3: Does the number match in every telling?
One school district's saving, four sources:

- $250k (April)
- $220k (May)
- $250k again (late May)
- No dollar figure at all (a fourth interview)

The same interview puts the tooling spend at about $100 a month, where earlier
tellings said $200.

---

### Slide 5: Check 4: What is the saving a percentage of?
Over roughly the same stretch, that board approved a $4.8 million four-year
Apple lease.

- The saving everyone wrote about is around 4.6% of one hardware contract
- The district's own follow-up: "The dollars were never the point. The precedent was."
- I agree with him. It's still worth knowing the denominator.

---

### Slide 6: Check 5: What did it replace, and who built that?
A CIO replacing packaged software with in-house builds, about $50,000 a year saved
on a finance app.

- An earlier vendor customer story says $60,000
- Different department, and "30 days to 2 hours" vs "30 days to a few days"
- That vendor page also names what was being replaced: a 15-year-old in-house
  service bus whose original developers are long gone
- The in-house build replacing the SaaS is replacing an in-house build that rotted

---

### Slide 7: Check 6: Is the person who built it still there?
A COO posted in April that he'd built an internal HR objectives tool himself and
avoided close to $100,000 in software.

- A commenter warned about "the long-term maintenance cost, which compounds over time"
- Seven weeks later he announced he was leaving after 13 years
- Nobody has said the tool broke
- Nobody has said anything about it at all, and there's no public record of who has it now

---

### Slide 8: Check 7: Is there a number in it dated after year one?
Not in a single case I checked. Including the two I'd cited myself.

- Every failure I could verify surfaced because something forced it out
- A £239m government contract priced on 95% automation, unpicked in front of a
  parliamentary committee that could compel testimony
- Under 3% of one office's AI-generated bill summaries met standard, out in
  congressional testimony
- Committees, arbitrators, regulators. Without one of those, nobody publishes.

---

### Slide 9: Close
**One check to keep**

If the number has no date after year one, it isn't evidence yet. It's a launch post.

Save this for your next build-versus-buy meeting.
