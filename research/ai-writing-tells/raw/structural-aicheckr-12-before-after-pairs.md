# AI Slop Examples: 12 Before-and-After Fixes You Can Steal

Definitions of AI slop are everywhere; examples you can actually calibrate your eye against are rarer. Below are twelve patterns we see constantly in 2026-era output from GPT-5, Claude, and Gemini — each shown as the slop version, why it fails, and a human rewrite that fixes it. If you want the full background first, start with our hub guide, what AI slop is and how to detect it. None of these tells is proof of AI on its own; slop is what happens when they stack.

### 1. Em-dash abuse

Current models lean on em dashes to splice clauses that should be separate sentences — several per paragraph is one of the most recognizable 2026 fingerprints.

AI slop

Remote work isn't going anywhere — it's evolving. Companies are adapting — some faster than others — and the ones that embrace flexibility — truly embrace it — will win the talent war.

Human fix

Remote work is evolving, not disappearing. Some companies are adapting faster than others. The ones offering real flexibility, not a hybrid policy nobody asked for, are winning the hiring battles we've watched this year.

### 2. "Delve" and the tapestry vocabulary

Certain words are massively overrepresented in LLM output: delve, tapestry, landscape, realm, crucial, pivotal, seamless. Humans use them occasionally; models reach for them constantly.

AI slop

Let's delve into the rich tapestry of the modern marketing landscape, where navigating the digital realm has become crucial for brands seeking seamless engagement.

Human fix

Marketing changed again this year. Three shifts matter for most brands: search traffic fell, email got cheaper to personalize, and short video stopped being optional. Here's what to do about each.

### 3. The generic macro-intro

Openings that gesture at "today's world" instead of the actual topic. They could preface any article ever written — which is exactly the problem.

AI slop

In today's fast-paced digital world, artificial intelligence is transforming the way we live and work. From healthcare to finance, AI is revolutionizing industries at an unprecedented pace.

Human fix

Our support team closed 41% of tickets with no human involvement last quarter. A year ago that number was zero. This is what changed, what broke, and what we'd do differently.

### 4. Hedging overload

Models are trained to avoid overcommitting, so untouched output qualifies everything. Text that never takes a position reads as slop because it can't be wrong — or useful.

AI slop

This approach might potentially help some teams improve productivity in certain contexts, though results may vary depending on various factors, and it could be worth considering whether it aligns with your specific needs.

Human fix

This works for teams under ten people. Past that, the standup format collapses under its own weight — we watched it happen twice — and you're better off with async written updates.

### 5. The "it's not just X, it's Y" formula

A contrast template that models deploy for fake profundity. One instance is fine; slop text uses it as a load-bearing structure every few paragraphs.

AI slop

This isn't just a product update — it's a paradigm shift. We're not simply adding features; we're reimagining what's possible. It's not about the technology — it's about the people.

Human fix

The update adds two things users kept asking for: offline mode and CSV export. Offline mode took eight months because sync conflicts are genuinely hard. Here's how we handled them.

### 6. Listicle filler

Slop lists restate the heading in different words instead of adding information. Each bullet is grammatically distinct and informationally identical.

AI slop

Benefits of drinking water: 1) Hydration is essential for your body. 2) Staying hydrated supports overall wellness. 3) Water helps maintain your body's hydration levels. 4) Proper hydration is key to health.

Human fix

What water actually changes: afternoon headaches often trace to mild dehydration; you routinely mistake thirst for hunger; and most adults do fine on roughly 2-3 liters a day from all sources — the '8 glasses' rule was never a research finding.

### 7. Rule-of-three adjective stacking

Models love triplets: fast, reliable, and efficient. The rhythm feels polished, but three near-synonyms carry the information of one.

AI slop

Our platform is powerful, robust, and scalable. The interface is clean, intuitive, and user-friendly. Setup is quick, easy, and seamless.

Human fix

Setup takes about four minutes — you paste one script tag. The dashboard loads in under a second on a 50k-row dataset, which is the thing our old tool couldn't do.

### 8. Conclusion boilerplate

"In conclusion, it is important to note..." endings that summarize the summary. If the conclusion could be deleted with zero information loss, it's slop.

AI slop

In conclusion, AI detection is a complex and evolving field. It is important to note that there are many factors to consider. Ultimately, the choice depends on your unique needs and circumstances.

Human fix

If you check one document a week, use a free sentence-level checker and move on. If you grade essays for a living, add a second tool and never act on one score alone. That's the whole decision.

### 9. "Furthermore/Moreover" transition chains

Formal connectives standing in for actual logical connection. Human writers mostly connect ideas through content; slop connects them through vocabulary.

AI slop

Furthermore, social media presence is important. Moreover, consistency plays a key role. Additionally, engagement should not be overlooked. It is also worth noting that analytics matter.

Human fix

Posting consistently beats posting brilliantly — an account shipping three decent posts a week outgrows one dropping a masterpiece a month. The catch: consistency only compounds if you read your analytics and cut what isn't working.

### 10. Fake specificity and orphan statistics

Numbers with no source, no year, no context. Slop borrows the texture of evidence without the substance — and the stats are frequently hallucinated outright.

AI slop

Studies show that 73% of businesses now use AI. Research indicates productivity increases by up to 40%. Experts agree this trend will accelerate.

Human fix

McKinsey's 2025 state-of-AI survey put enterprise AI adoption at 72% — up from 55% a year earlier. Productivity claims vary too much by task to average into one number, which is why we measured our own: 22% faster ticket resolution, flat for code review.

### 11. The "whether you're a..." audience hedge

An opener that tries to address everyone and therefore addresses no one. It's the written equivalent of a form letter.

AI slop

Whether you're a seasoned professional or just starting your journey, whether you run a Fortune 500 company or a small startup, this guide has something for everyone.

Human fix

This guide is for freelancers who invoice under $10k a month and do their own bookkeeping. If you have an accountant, skip to the audit section — the rest will bore you.

### 12. Restating the question as the answer

Answer-shaped text that explains why the question matters instead of answering it. Common in AI-generated FAQs and answer-box content.

AI slop

How much does it cost? Pricing is an important consideration when choosing any tool. Costs can vary based on your needs, usage, and the features you require. It's worth evaluating your budget carefully.

Human fix

It costs $12/month billed annually, or $18 month-to-month. The free tier covers 5 checks a day, which is enough for most students. Teams pay per seat with a 20% break at ten seats.

## The Pattern Behind All Twelve

Every example above fails the same way: the slop version could have been written without knowing anything. No numbers that could be wrong, no positions that could be argued with, no details that could only come from doing the work. The human fixes aren't stylistically fancier — most are plainer — they're just specific. That's the transferable lesson: you fix slop by adding information, not adjectives.

It's also why slop and AI detection overlap so heavily. Uniform rhythm, predictable vocabulary, and low-information sentences are exactly the statistical signals detectors measure. Fix the slop and you usually move the score.

## FAQ

### What does AI slop look like in practice?

AI slop reads smooth but says nothing: em-dash-heavy sentences, vocabulary like 'delve,' 'tapestry,' and 'landscape,' hedging that never commits ('might potentially help'), symmetrical 'it's not just X, it's Y' constructions, and intros like 'In today's fast-paced world.' Each pattern is fine once; slop is what happens when they stack in every paragraph.

### What words are signs of AI slop?

The most reliable 2026 tells are 'delve,' 'tapestry,' 'landscape' (metaphorical), 'crucial,' 'pivotal,' 'seamless,' 'robust,' 'leverage,' 'elevate,' 'navigate' (metaphorical), 'in the realm of,' and 'it's important to note.' None of these prove AI on their own — humans use them too — but three or more per paragraph is a strong signal.

### How do I fix AI slop in my own writing?

Replace abstractions with specifics (a number, a name, a date), cut hedges and commit to a claim, break symmetrical sentence structures, delete filler transitions, and read it aloud — slop survives skimming but not speech. A humanizer can automate the first pass; the specifics still have to come from you.

### Does fixing AI slop help with AI detection?

Usually, yes. The patterns that make text read as slop — uniform sentence rhythm, predictable word choice, low specificity — are close cousins of the statistical signals AI detectors flag. Rewriting for specificity and varied rhythm tends to lower AI scores. Verify by running the rewrite through a sentence-level checker rather than assuming.

### Is every em dash or 'delve' AI slop?

No. Human writers used em dashes and 'delve' long before ChatGPT. Slop is a density problem, not a single-word problem: one em dash is style, five in a paragraph is a fingerprint. Judge the pattern across the whole text — ideally with sentence-level analysis, not word-spotting.
