You might remember the bank that replaced a bunch of staff with AI and then had to give them their jobs back. Almost nobody knows what happened a year later.

August 2025: Commonwealth Bank in Australia cut 45 roles, saying a new voice bot had reduced call volumes by 2,000 a week. The union said volumes were actually going up, and that team leaders were being pulled onto the phones to cope. The bank admitted its assessment "did not adequately consider all relevant business considerations" and hired for those roles again.

What got much less attention is that a completely separate system was quietly getting good. Its chat assistant launched in late 2024, and by May this year it was handling almost nine in ten customer conversations on its own.

Then in July the job losses turned up somewhere else entirely. Hundreds of roles went at Nutun, the Johannesburg contractor that had been running the bank's online chat.

Those 45 kept their jobs because they were bank staff, covered by an agreement, with a union that took the bank to a tribunal until it backed down. The contractors had none of that, and were never on the bank's headcount to begin with.

So the cost came out in the end. It came out of the outsourced tier.
