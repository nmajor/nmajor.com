## https://sfstandard.com/2026/05/11/ai-detection-cheating-palo-alto/ (SF Standard, 2026-05-11) — WebFetch SUMMARY, not verbatim
A Palo Alto High School sophomore faced academic penalties after an AI-detection tool flagged his essay. The family filed a federal civil rights lawsuit in May 2026 alleging discriminatory application of the detection software.
Incident: student submitted an essay on Arthur Miller's "The Crucible" in October. By December, teacher Sarah Bartlett flagged it using Turnitin, which reported "76% of it had likely been written using AI." The student was required to rewrite the essay in class and received a D, reducing his semester grade from a high B or low A to a C.
Family evidence: multiple drafts, Google Doc revision timestamps, a 1,162-page evidentiary packet submitted in January. They offered a compromise (accept a B). The district declined in April 2026.
Lawsuit: filed May 5, 2026 in the Northern District of California. Complaint alleges "Male students in Bartlett's class were four to five times more likely than female students to be subjected to the Turnitin assessment." At least one other Asian student received identical treatment. Alleges Title IX and Title VI discrimination. Alleges parents were not notified despite school policy requirements.
Relief sought: grade restoration, record expungement, compensatory damages, and a court order prohibiting AI detection scores as sole evidence without educator review.
Turnitin's own disclaimer quoted: "Our AI writing detection model may not always be accurate — it may misidentify human-written, AI-generated, and AI-paraphrased text — so it should not be used as the sole basis for adverse actions against a student."
Margin of error cited: ±15 percentage points. Stanford research cited: "neurodivergent students and students for whom English is a second language are flagged at significantly higher rates." Grammarly use can trigger false positives.
Similar cases cited nationwide: University of Houston, University of Buffalo, University of Minnesota.

## https://www.timeshighereducation.com/news/students-win-plagiarism-appeals-over-generative-ai-detection-tool — WebFetch SUMMARY. PUBLICATION DATE: July 15, 2025 (OLD — context only)
Office of the Independent Adjudicator (OIA) for England and Wales upheld multiple student complaints:
1. Student with autism given a zero after detection software flagged the essay; on reconsideration "there had not been any academic misconduct."
2. International student appealed after Turnitin identified "substantial amounts" of AI-generated content; student admitted using Grammarly; OIA found the student "had not been given a fair opportunity to respond."
3. International student found guilty after Turnitin flagged AI content; claimed to have used only Google for synonyms; OIA noted the university "did not consider whether Turnitin's AI detection might be less reliable for non-native English speakers."
4. Postgraduate failed after Turnitin flagged hallucinated references; university agreed to reconsider after the OIA expressed concern about lack of detailed reasoning.
OIA guidance quote: the responsibility "is on the provider to prove that the student has done what they are accused of doing, not on the student to disprove it."
