Rockwell says it cut average machine repair time from 18 minutes to 12.

The company did a lot more than put manuals behind a chatbot. It interviewed technicians with 20 to 30 years on the factory floor, organized what they knew by symptom, cause and response, then combined that material with manuals, maintenance history and factory data.

And it put the interface beside the machine. A technician can ask by text or voice on a tablet while the equipment is down.

Rockwell also reports 33% less downtime and a drop from nine months to three for troubleshooting proficiency. Those are company figures without a published measurement method, so I would treat them as reported outcomes.

I think the tablet beside the broken machine matters as much as the model. Knowledge people cannot reach in the moment is still missing from the job.
