# Commonwealth Bank of Australia — AI "voice-bot" call-centre deployment reversed, 45 redundancies rescinded

FETCHED: 2026-07-20
SOURCE TYPE: Primary + strong secondary. CBA issued its own on-record admission (quoted by ABC and ACS Information Age); the dispute went to the Fair Work Commission (a real tribunal record). AFR and Bloomberg covered independently. Union (FSU) is an interested party — separate its framing from CBA's own quotes.

## Verbatim — ABC News (21 Aug 2025)

> Commonwealth Bank announced 45 job cuts last month, as it introduced an AI "voice-bot", but has now reversed its decision.
> The bank has apologised to affected employees for the "error", but the Finance Sector Union says the "damage is already done" to workers.
> CBA says workers can choose to stay in their roles, be redeployed or leave the bank. Another Fair Work hearing is scheduled for next week.

> The Commonwealth Bank has backtracked on dozens of job cuts, describing its decision to axe 45 roles due to artificial intelligence as an "error".
> CBA said it had apologised to the affected employees after finding the customer service roles were not redundant despite introducing an AI-powered "voice-bot".
> The Finance Sector Union has described the reversal as a "major win", after it raised a dispute at the Fair Work Commission.

> It said its members found work actually spiked after the bot was introduced, despite the bank's claims it would reduce calls.
> "Our investment in technology, including AI, is making it easier and faster for customers to get help, especially in our call centres," CBA had said when confirming the job cuts in July.
> The FSU said the experience of workers after the bot was introduced was "a very different story".
> "Call volumes were rising, with management scrambling to offer overtime and even pulling team leaders onto the phones," its statement read.
> CBA admitted it "did not adequately consider all relevant business considerations" when announcing the redundancies and acknowledged "we should have been more thorough in our assessment of the roles required."

## Verbatim — CBA's own statement, via Information Age / ACS

> CBA confirmed to Information Age on Thursday the decision that the 45 customer service jobs were no longer needed was incorrect.
> "CBA's initial assessment that the 45 roles in our Customer Service Direct business were not required did not adequately consider all relevant business considerations and this error meant the roles were not redundant," a spokesperson said.
> "We have apologised to the employees concerned and acknowledge we should have been more thorough in our assessment of the roles required."

> At the time, a CBA spokesperson said "some roles and work can change" due to the use of technology, and the organisation would "review the skills we need and how we're organised to deliver the best customer experiences and outcomes".

## Real numbers
- 45 customer-service roles cut, then rescinded (union claims 90 total eliminated in the wider round).
- CBA's claim: voice bot reduced call volumes by 2,000 per week. Reality per staff/FSU: call volumes ROSE; overtime offered; team leaders pulled onto phones.
- Timeline: cuts announced ~28 Jul 2025 → FSU dispute at Fair Work Commission → reversal + apology 20-21 Aug 2025. Roughly three weeks from announcement to reversal.

## Caveats / gaps
- CBA is ~50,000 staff — NOT mid-market. Included because the numbers are real and the admission is on the record from the company itself.
- CBA never said the voice bot itself was withdrawn — only that the redundancies were an error. This is a *reversal of the headcount decision*, not a documented decommissioning of the AI tool. Do not overstate it as "CBA scrapped its AI bot."
- The "2,000 calls a week" figure is CBA's own pre-deployment claim, reported secondhand. Should be verified against AFR/Bloomberg primary text before being used in print.
