# Air Canada chatbot decision verification

Captured 2026-09-29.

Primary decision:
https://www.canlii.org/en/bc/bccrt/doc/2024/2024bccrt149/2024bccrt149.html

CanLII's own case roundup reproduces the decisive passages:
https://blog.canlii.org/tag/hotoncanlii/page/3/

Citation: *Moffatt v. Air Canada*, 2024 BCCRT 149, British Columbia Civil Resolution
Tribunal.

The tribunal found that Air Canada's website chatbot gave Jake Moffatt inaccurate
information about applying a bereavement fare retroactively. A separate Air Canada page
gave the correct policy. The tribunal rejected Air Canada's attempt to distinguish the
chatbot from the rest of its website and found that the airline had not taken reasonable
care to ensure the chatbot was accurate.

The decision awarded C$650.88 in damages. The order also included C$36.14 in
pre-judgment interest and C$125 in tribunal fees, for C$812.02 total.

Technology caveat: the decision calls the interface a chatbot but does not establish
that it used a large language model, generative AI, retrieval-augmented generation, or a
vector database. Do not call this an LLM hallucination. It verifies an information
governance and consistency failure.

Authority caveat: this is a published small-claims decision from the British Columbia
Civil Resolution Tribunal, not an appellate-court precedent or a general statement of
law outside the dispute.
