# Pitch pool — week of 2026-08-18
Issue: "Seven days to close. Years to know if it worked." (seven-days-to-close)
Concept: the countable-now metric is an input; the outcome metric arrives later and only if
somebody instrumented for it in advance.

Sources and verbatim excerpts for every pitch are in `raw/`.

## E1 (essay slice) — The bank that quantified the speed and declined to quantify the money
Customers Bancorp told investors an AI pilot closed commercial loans in seven days vs a stated
30-60 day norm, and reported 46,000 hours saved. A JP Morgan analyst asked what it had saved in
expense. The CEO said the tech was "only about six months old" and these things take "quarters
not weeks." Speed was countable in July. The result is not.

## E2 (essay slice) — Three lines on one page
The take-home: metric, owner, date. Plus the step that makes it possible, tagging the cases the
tool touches at the moment it touches them. A cohort you never marked is one you can't compare.

## P1 — "Not an announcement, this is a measurement"
q.beyond, 2026-08-10: 300 AI-automated workflows, 6,000+ hours saved per month (~40 FTEs), 10%+
efficiency in managed services. CFO Nora Wolters: "not an announcement, this is a measurement."
Same release cut FY26 EBITDA guidance from EUR 10-16M to EUR 3-7M. Managed services revenue, the
line where the 10% gain was claimed, fell 7.5% YoY. Stock -7.2%.
CONSTRAINT: management attributes the cut to weak SME demand, not AI. Never imply causation.

## P2 — The regulator wrote agentic AI out of the rulebook (found independently by 2 desks)
SR 11-7 (2011) made outcomes analysis mandatory and prescribed early-warning metrics for exactly
the long-horizon problem. On 2026-04-17 SR 26-2 / OCC Bulletin 2026-13 superseded it: generative
and agentic AI "are not within the scope of this guidance," and it disclaims enforceability.
Sidhu's phrase: "a multi-agentic credit underwriting process."
NOTE: now also covered in the essay itself, so a post must add something the essay does not.

## P4 — 13% of the cases, 73% of the money, and only 2 of 15 are about USING AI
Cornerstone/Stanford 2026 midyear: 15 AI-related filings H1 2026, 13% of core filings, but $385bn
DDL (73%). Only 2 of 15 coded "AI Usage." The suits hit companies that SELL AI, not use it.
Angle: nobody external is coming for a mid-market lender's soft AI number, which is why the
discipline has to be internal.

## P5 — The 95% number turns one year old today
Fortune published MIT NANDA's "95% of GenAI pilots fail" on 2025-08-18, one year ago today.
Pair with Standish CHAOS (16% success, 1994), which Eveleens & Verhoef reproduced in 2010 on
5,457 forecasts across 1,211 projects: a best-in-class org scored 35%. Standish scored estimate
adherence, an input, and called it success. Verdict: "misleading, one-sided... meaningless figures."
When nobody instruments the outcome, the number that fills the gap is one nobody can audit.

## P6 — 61% mentioned speed. 98% claimed accuracy. 9% disclosed ROI.
Oshri & Moore reviewed 155 published RPA reports 2014-2019. A decade before a bank said seven days
and declined to say what it saved.
CONSTRAINT: measures DISCLOSURE IN MEDIA REPORTS, not implementations. Frame precisely or drop.

## P7 — Stanford wrote the tagging step as actual plumbing
Stanford Health Care's DEPLOYR writes an inference packet at the moment of inference (identifier,
timestamp, features, output); a scheduled LabelExtractor returns later and pairs it to the real
outcome. Running twelve models silently for five weeks, prospective AUROC fell on ALL twelve vs
retrospective (sodium 0.79 -> 0.71). Shadow mode gives you the cohort for free.

## P8 — We already ran this experiment, and faster loans defaulted less
Fuster, Plosser, Schnabl & Vickery (RFS 32(5), 2019): fintech lenders cut processing ~10 days,
"faster processing does not come at the cost of higher defaults," and on FHA loans default rates
were "about 25% lower." Readable at 12 and 24 months.
This complicates our own argument. Publishing it is the trust move.
CONSTRAINT: mortgages not C&I/CRE; 2010-2016 benign period; rules-based, not agentic.

## P10 — The hours you saved were spent before anyone counted them
Hours saved is a metric that deletes itself. Ramp's Ara Kharazian: "These metrics say nothing about
the quality of work being produced." Operators describe the saved hour going to review, waiting, or
nothing. Honest follow-up to Bancorp's 46,000 hours: did the headcount change?
CONSTRAINT: anonymous HN voices, directional only. Meta/Boz item is second-hand, unverified.
The circulating "3% hours saved" study is unverified. Do not cite.
