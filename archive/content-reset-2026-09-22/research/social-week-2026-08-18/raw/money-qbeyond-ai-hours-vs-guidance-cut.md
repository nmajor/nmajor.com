## WHY THIS IS ON-CONCEPT

The essay's argument: companies report the metric that is countable NOW (an INPUT - hours, cycle time, seats, adoption) because the OUTCOME metric takes a cycle to exist.

q.beyond is the same pattern with the outcome number visible in the same document:
- INPUT metrics, precise and celebrated: 300 workflows, 6,000+ hours/month, ~40 FTE-equivalents, "10%+" efficiency, and a CFO explicitly framing it as measured rather than announced ("not an announcement, this is a measurement").
- OUTCOME metric, in the same release, moving the other way: FY EBITDA guidance cut from EUR 10-16M to EUR 3-7M; revenue guidance cut; managed services revenue -7.5% YoY - and managed services is the exact business line where the 10%+ AI efficiency gain was claimed.
- The savings that ARE quantified are FUTURE and CONDITIONAL: ~EUR 7M annually "from 2027", against EUR 5-6M of one-off transformation cost now.
- The CEO quote concedes the split directly: AI progress "as planned", demand weak.

Nothing here says the AI work is fake or that the guidance cut was caused by AI. The point is the reporting shape: the input metric is instrumented to the hour; the outcome metric is a forward-dated promise.

## LIMITS / WEAKNESSES
- Every AI figure is COMPANY-REPORTED, from the company's own slides and its own ad-hoc release. No independent verification.
- q.beyond is a small German IT services company (~EUR 180M revenue, Frankfurt-listed, ticker QBY). It is not a household name to a US/UK audience.
- The company sells AI services; its AI claims are also marketing to its own customers. Flag as vendor-adjacent.
- The guidance cut is attributed by management to weak SME demand plus transformation one-offs, NOT to AI underperformance. Do not assert causation.
- Primary fetch was of an EQS release mirrored on TradingView, not qbeyond.de directly (the IR news page returned 404 on fetch).
- The 70-80 German job cuts figure is single-sourced via search summary; the ad-hoc release itself gives no number.
