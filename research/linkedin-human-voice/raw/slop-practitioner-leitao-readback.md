# LinkedIn (kinda) answered our questions about the new "AI slop" reporting feature: | Paulina Leitão · LinkedIn · 2026-07-31

**Paulina Leitão**: Brand & Executive Content Strategist at Trans.eu Group for 9 months with 14 years 6 months of total professional experience. Previous roles include Marketing Lead at Milo Solutions, Website Copywriter & Content Manager at Milo Solutions, and Senior Communications & Product Marketing Manager at Survicate. Based in Wrocław, Dolnośląskie, Poland [PL].

---

LinkedIn (kinda) answered our questions about the new "AI slop" reporting feature:

→ If someone reports your content 🚩 - it'll be flagged in your own analytics dashboard, private to you only. This is so you realize people feel your posts are inauthentic and change your bad, bad behavior. LinkedIn believes humans giving humans feedback on that - rather than just having an AI detector decide whether your content is AI-generated (wait, what? 😅) - is better (and you know - more human.) This little system isn't there yet, but it will be "tested."

→ The "Seems like AI slop" button is meant for humans to help train LinkedIn's automated classifiers on what is and isn't AI slop. They say it's hard to define - so having actual members flag it will help them "make better feeds."

→ They realized it was a little hypocritical, so the "Enhance your post with AI" is being removed and replaced with a proofreading feature that doesn't change your voice.

→ Using AI to fix errors and smooth the flow ≠ handing AI the wheel entirely. So if your last step before publishing a post is asking Claude to review your writing - you're good.

→ Will we be able to report AI comments too? Not exactly - they didn't mention a report button for comments, only that they're already working on catching and blocking them automatically.
