## Sweep 1 — Tokenmaxxing: the leaderboard did it every time

**Hook:** Amazon's AI leaderboard drove engineers to point agents at junk tasks to climb it. There's a name for this now: tokenmaxxing.

**Story.** Leaked internal metrics (Tom's Hardware / gHacks, Jul 30-31) show a Claude
Sonnet author-matching job at Amazon ran five months undetected and 860% over budget,
$1.8M on a single task, plus a $541K and a $134K overrun elsewhere. The mechanism is the
story: Amazon set an 80%-weekly-AI-usage goal plus a leaderboard, engineers assigned
agents to junk work to climb the rankings, and an SVP had to say out loud "Please don't
use AI just for the sake of using AI." Amazon killed the leaderboard in late May. Meta ran
its own token leaderboard, internally named "Claudeonomics" — 85,000+ staff ranked,
"Token Legend" titles, ~$221M of tokens in one month. Every blowout in the window traces
to an input metric someone was ranked on.

**Sources:** r/technology 2026-07-30 (23.5k upvotes) → Tom's Hardware/gHacks leaked
metrics; MLQ + IBTimes UK on Meta "Claudeonomics"; all in raw/last30days/. NOTE: leaked
internal metrics reported at one remove — attribute to the outlets, and the Meta numbers
need a verification fetch before drafting.

**Why now:** the Amazon leak is 5 days old and was the single biggest social item in the
sweep; it's the story everyone will have half-seen this week.

**First-person angle:** Nick's essay line extended — the scoreboard was the bill — but at
two more companies, with the gaming behavior made explicit and named. He can say: if you
rank people on an input, they will manufacture the input; the fix is owning the output
metric before the meter starts.

**Breakout:** high — "tokenmaxxing" is a sticky coinage riding the window's biggest story.
Redundancy risk with the essay and with essay-slice 1 (both are leaderboard stories);
if this runs, essay-slice 1 probably shouldn't, or must run issue-day before this one.

## Sweep 2 — Uber's second act: caps didn't fix it, so now there are pods

**Hook:** Uber capped AI spend at $1,500 a month per engineer. Then it quietly started
embedding AI engineers in finance and HR instead.

**Story.** The June cap ($1,500/engineer/month per tool) was the spending answer. The
August move is the interesting one: "Agentic Pods" — the CTO embeds top AI engineers in
finance, HR, legal, and marketing for two-week sprints, sitting with the team first to
find the workflow before any tool gets pointed at it (newsbytesapp 2026-08-03, X coverage
2026-08-02). Read together: the cap answered "how much," the pods are the first attempt at
"what for" — moving the effort from metering usage to picking the work. Bonus tension for
comments: a data-driven X take (Jul 17, top engagement in the sweep) argues Uber is
doubling down, not retreating — agentic adoption 32%→84% in two months.

**Sources:** newsbytesapp.com 2026-08-03; @LayoffAI 2026-08-02; witho2.com 2026-07-10;
@stevenfiorillo 2026-07-17; all in raw/last30days/. NOTE: pods coverage is thin/secondary
so far — needs a verification fetch before drafting; if it doesn't verify, drop.

**Why now:** days old; it's the natural sequel to today's essay and nobody has written the
"caps answer how-much, pods attempt what-for" frame yet.

**First-person angle:** the pods pattern is literally what Nick does as a consultant — sit
with the team, find the workflow, then bring the tool. He can bless the pattern while
staying honest that Uber still hasn't shown an output.

**Breakout:** medium-high — a genuine second-act scoop angle on a story everyone knows.
Redundancy risk with the essay itself: this MUST NOT run issue day; it works best late in
the week as the follow-up.
