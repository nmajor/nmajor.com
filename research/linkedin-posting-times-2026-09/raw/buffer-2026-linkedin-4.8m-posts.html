The Best Time to Post on LinkedIn in 2026: 4.8 Million Posts Analyzed

LinkedIn

PublishedSep 9, 2026

We analyzed over 4.8 million posts sent through Buffer to pinpoint the best time and day to post on LinkedIn for maximum engagement.

Reading time
13 minute read

After digging into millions of posts sent through Buffer, we've pinpointed the best time to post on all major social platforms, LinkedIn included.

By "we," I mean Julian Winternheimer, our resident data scientist.

We've (OK, Julian has) been running these numbers for around two years now, and the 2026 LinkedIn findings surprised me a little: The best time to post on LinkedIn in 2026 is generally between 3 p.m. and 8 p.m. on weekdays, with Wednesday at 4 p.m. emerging as the strongest overall slot in our analysis of 4.8 million posts.

In 2025, peak engagement was almost entirely confined to working hours — things fell off pretty sharply once 5 p.m. hit. This year, we're seeing those peak windows shift later into the day, which mirrors what's been happening on Instagram and TikTok for a while now.

Late afternoon and evening hours are now pulling some of the strongest LinkedIn engagement numbers we've seen. People are still showing up for professional content; they're just doing it after they've closed their laptops for the day.

But I'm getting ahead of myself. Let's get into the data.

Key takeaways

  • Peak hours: Late afternoon and evening hours (3 p.m.–8 p.m.) now drive the highest engagement on LinkedIn, although some days have high-performing slots later in the evening.
  • Top slots: Wednesday at 4 p.m., Friday at 3 p.m., and Friday at 4 p.m. are the highest-performing times.
  • Best day: Wednesday is the best day to post, closely followed by Thursday and Friday.
  • Early-week dip: Monday and Tuesday see the lowest engagement; save your best content for mid-to-late week.

Is there a best time to post on LinkedIn?

Yes, our data shows that when you post on LinkedIn can meaningfully impact how much engagement your content gets.

The best time to post on LinkedIn is generally 3 p.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays. Wednesday at 4 p.m. is the strongest overall time in our analysis, followed by Friday at 3 p.m. and 4 p.m.

That said, as even LinkedIn's own marketing blog points out, timing research isn't a black-and-white science, and every audience is a little different.

After analyzing more than 4.8 million LinkedIn posts sent through Buffer, we were able to pinpoint some clear posting windows when content tended to get higher engagement than others.

Your LinkedIn content is more likely to get engagement in these windows, whether you're looking to grow a LinkedIn Page for a brand or your own personal brand with a LinkedIn profile.

Engagement is a particularly useful benchmark when it comes to identifying the best time to post on LinkedIn. The platform’s algorithm uses interactions (reactions, comments, and reposts) as signals to help it decide which posts are valuable and should be shown to more people.

In other words, the more engagement your post receives, the more people will see your post. That's a simplification, as loads of factors go into how posts are ranked, but it's a solid rule of thumb. Strong engagement doesn't automatically guarantee more reach, but the two definitely influence each other.

This is why we consider time slots in which posts got the highest engagement to be the best time to post on LinkedIn.

But here's the thing — every audience is a little different.

My recommendation? Treat these times as a starting point, experiment with your own posting schedule as part of your LinkedIn marketing strategy, and dig into your social media analytics to find what works best for your specific audience and make the most of the LinkedIn algorithm.

The best time to post on LinkedIn for engagement

The best time to post on LinkedIn for high engagement is generally during late afternoon and evening hours on weekdays. The top-performing slots include:

  • Peak window: 3 p.m. – 8 p.m.
  • Top slot 1: Wednesday at 4 p.m.
  • Top slot 2: Friday at 3 p.m.
  • Top slot 3: Friday at 4 p.m.

You can see it all pretty clearly in the graph above. It breaks down the week into hourly chunks, where the darkest blocks are the time slots with the highest engagement (and the best time to post on LinkedIn), and the light to white blocks are the time slots with the lowest engagement.

The shift toward evening engagement on LinkedIn looks a little different from what we saw in 2025, when the best posting times were exclusively within working hours. What's interesting is that this late-day activity doesn't seem to be passive scrolling. Professionals winding down their workday are often reading thought leadership, exploring solutions, and engaging with peers more intentionally — which could mean higher-quality interactions for your content, too.

That doesn't mean midday is a dead zone, though. LinkedIn's own marketing blog notes that pockets of the workday (like the lunch break or mid-afternoon) are still moments when professionals pause to scroll. In our data, those midday hours perform decently; they're just no longer the clear peak the way they were in 2025.

As you'll see from the graph above, engagement picks up in the afternoon and remains strong through the evening hours. Unlike other social platforms where evening slots have always performed well, this represents a notable shift in LinkedIn behavior.

Posts shared in the morning hours (6 a.m.–11 a.m.) now tend to see lower engagement than afternoon and evening slots. If your current posting schedule is built entirely around morning windows, it might be worth testing some later time slots to see if your audience has shifted, too.

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Monday

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Monday is 10 p.m., as professionals catch up on content after their workday. If you're looking for more Monday slots to add to your posting schedule, LinkedIn posts shared at 5 p.m. and 9 p.m. also saw higher engagement than the rest of the day.

Monday overall tends to see lower engagement compared to midweek, so consider saving your highest-priority content for Wednesday through Friday.

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Tuesday

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Tuesday is 4 p.m., as users begin wrapping up their workday and checking their feeds. If you need more posting times to choose from, 5 p.m. is not far off in terms of engagement, as is 10 p.m.

Like Monday, Tuesday sees slightly lower overall engagement, so test these times with your audience before locking in a regular Tuesday schedule.

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Wednesday

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Wednesday is 4 p.m. This is actually one of the highest-engagement time slots of the entire week. Other top posting times for Wednesday are 3 p.m. and 5 p.m., giving you a solid afternoon-to-early-evening window.

Wednesday is your best day overall for LinkedIn engagement, so this is a great day to share your most important content.

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Thursday

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Thursday is 5 p.m. Posts shared during the late afternoon and evening hours tend to perform well, so this time is definitely worth adding to your content calendar. If you need more times on Thursday, 7 p.m. is also strong, and 9 p.m. is worth considering for reaching users who engage later in the evening.

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Friday

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Friday is 3 p.m., right as professionals are starting to wind down their week. Other strong contenders for your posting schedule are 4 p.m. and 5 p.m.

Friday is one of your strongest days for engagement, and definitely worth keeping in your rotation.

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Saturday

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Saturday is 9 a.m. Posts shared on Saturday tend to see less engagement on the platform than most weekdays, so consider scheduling your content for a weekday instead. If you must post on Saturday, our data showed that 9 a.m. tends to get the highest engagement, followed by 6 p.m. and 10 p.m.

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Sunday

The best time to post on LinkedIn on Sunday is 10 p.m. Still, content shared on Sundays sees lower engagement compared to weekdays, and this slot is definitely not your best option if you're hoping to boost post interactions. If Sunday works better for your schedule, late evening is your best bet. Other times to consider are 9 p.m. and 6 a.m.

Cross-platform timing: Just as there's no one-size-fits-all best time to post, there's no single best time to post across all social platforms. Here's the best time to post on Instagram, the best time to post on Facebook, and the best time to post on TikTok.

The best day to post on LinkedIn

The best day of the week to post on LinkedIn is Wednesday. We found that posts shared on Wednesday tended to get the most engagement, closely followed by Thursday, then Friday.

Anecdotally, this mid-to-late-week pattern makes sense. By Wednesday, most people are fully in the swing of the week — focused, open to learning, but not yet counting down to Friday.

Monday and Tuesday, on the other hand, showed the lowest engagement in our data. Monday sees the weakest numbers of the week, with Tuesday not far behind.

It's worth noting that not all research agrees here. LinkedIn's marketing blog highlights studies where Tuesday is actually one of the most active days on the platform. This is a good reminder that results can vary depending on the dataset, audience, and industry, so it's worth testing early-week posts with your own followers before writing those days off entirely.

The heads-down work theory still holds, though: if your audience tends to be deep in tasks on Monday and Tuesday, mid-to-late week is likely your better bet.

Get the best times to post on LinkedIn right in Buffer

With Buffer, you can have your posting schedule automatically set to the best times. That goes for all the other channels that Buffer supports, too. They'll appear as posting slots in your queue and calendar, making it super easy for you to schedule posts at the best times.

It'll take you less than a minute to set up:

  1. Select LinkedIn on your channels list on the left.
  2. Click on the settings (gear) icon above the calendar.
  3. Scroll to Posting Schedule and under Posting Slots, click the Generate New Posting Slots button.
  4. Then Use Recommended Posting Slots.

The schedule will automatically update to include the best posting times for the week, based on our latest analysis of millions of LinkedIn posts.

The next time you schedule a post, you can click one of these high-engagement slots in your calendar or list view, or just hit Next available to add it to the next available slot.

How to find the best time to post on LinkedIn for your audience

The best times above are a great starting point if you're still figuring out your LinkedIn strategy. Still, they're no substitute for figuring out your own best time to post.

LinkedIn's native analytics show your top-performing posts over a set period (for both pages and profiles) so you can start spotting patterns and figure out your personal best time to post on LinkedIn.

However, you'll need a record of exactly when you posted a specific piece of content (like a social media management tool or social media content calendar) to refer back to.

LinkedIn Analytics won't show you detailed timings on published content after the fact. The best the LinkedIn timestamp will tell you is "3h" or "2d" (to indicate you posted the content three hours or two days ago, etc.)

This is where a tool like Buffer can help. When you schedule and publish your LinkedIn posts through Buffer, you get a clear record of exactly when each post went live, plus analytics that show you how each one performed. Over time, you'll start to spot patterns that reveal your unique best posting windows.

It's also worth noting that timing isn't the only variable to experiment with. The type of content you post can also affect how your audience responds at different times of day. So as you test different time slots, try mixing up your content formats, too.

Best time to post on LinkedIn FAQs

Here are some of the most commonly asked questions about when to post on LinkedIn.

Does the time you post on LinkedIn actually matter?

Our data, based on over 4.8 million LinkedIn posts, shows that yes, timing does make a difference. Posts shared during high-engagement windows (particularly late afternoon and evening hours on weekdays) consistently outperformed those posted at off-peak times.

Remember, timing isn't a magic fix. Posting at 4 p.m. on a Wednesday won't rescue a post that isn't relevant or valuable to your audience. Think of the top posting times as a helpful boost on top of a solid content strategy, not a substitute for one.

The good news? Acting on timing data is easier than ever. LinkedIn now lets you schedule posts directly within the platform, and tools like Buffer can automate your posting schedule based on high-engagement time slots so you don't have to remember to hit publish at exactly 4 p.m.

When is the worst time to post on LinkedIn?

Based on our data, the lowest-engagement windows are early in the week. Monday and Tuesday tend to underperform compared to mid-to-late week. Morning hours (before noon) also see weaker engagement now that late afternoon and evening have shifted to the top of the heatmap.

Weekends are generally quiet too, though Saturday at 9 a.m. and late Sunday evening can still see some activity. If you have a post you really want to perform, don't bury it on a Monday morning; save it for Wednesday through Friday instead.

What is the "golden hour" for LinkedIn posts?

The "golden hour" refers to the period right after a LinkedIn post goes live, when early engagement may help signal value to the algorithm and push the post to more people. LinkedIn hasn't confirmed an official 60- or 90-minute window, so treat it as a general concept rather than a hard rule.

That said, many creators try to be available to respond to comments shortly after posting. Engaging with early replies can keep the conversation going and may help maintain momentum during that initial window.

Is LinkedIn engagement higher in the morning or evening?

Our latest data shows that evening hours now outperform mornings on LinkedIn. Posts shared between 3 p.m. and 8 p.m. on weekdays consistently see higher engagement than those shared in the morning — a notable shift from what we saw in previous years, when traditional work hours dominated.

The theory? Professionals are increasingly catching up on LinkedIn content after work, during their commute home, or while unwinding in the evening. Our data can’t tell us why the change is happening, though, only that later posting times performed particularly well in this analysis.

What's the best time to post on LinkedIn on weekends?

Weekend engagement on LinkedIn drops off significantly compared to weekdays, so if you have the flexibility to schedule your posts for a weekday instead, that's likely the better move. If you do need to post on a weekend, Saturday at 9 a.m. is your best bet. Sunday late evening (around 10 p.m.) also shows some activity.

Do these posting times apply to my time zone?

Yes, our data scientist has crunched the numbers so that the recommended times are universally applicable. You don't need to convert anything. Whether you're posting from New York, London, or Mumbai, 4 p.m. means 4 p.m. in your local time zone.

How often should I post on LinkedIn?

Posting two to five times per week is where most creators and brands start to see results. Based on our data, accounts that post more consistently tend to see stronger impressions per post over time, and posting six to 10 times per week can push those numbers further still.

That said, consistency matters more than volume. It's better to post three times a week every week than to post daily for two weeks and then go quiet. Start with a schedule you can actually sustain, then build from there.

What type of content performs best on LinkedIn?

According to our data, carousel (document) posts generate up to 596% more engagement than text-only posts, making them the standout format on the platform right now. Beyond format, content that offers genuine insight, professional value, or a unique perspective tends to do well on LinkedIn.
