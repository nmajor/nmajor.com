LinkedIn has fundamentally changed from a chronological distribution system into a predictive recommendation engine… What does that mean? Five years ago, if you posted something, most of your reach happened quickly. Today, LinkedIn intentionally stretches content distribution over days and sometimes weeks. The problem for business people is obvious… Your content isn’t entertainment. You’re covering live events, trade shows, factory tours, product launches, industry announcements, and manufacturing news. News has a shelf life. Nobody wants to hear about a machine launch three weeks after the launch happened. Nobody wants to hear about Day 1 of a trade show when the trade show ended two weeks ago. Nobody wants “breaking news” after it has already become old news. The algorithm treats your trade show coverage the same way it treats a motivational quote, a selfie, or an AI-generated business post. That’s where the disconnect happens. The second issue is that AI has dramatically increased the volume of content competing for attention. Every day millions of additional posts are being generated across LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, X, and elsewhere. The average amount of content increased. Twenty years ago the challenge was creating content. Today the challenge is being seen. AI is absolutely disrupting attention, creativity, trust, search, education, marketing, social media, and many jobs. It is also making it easier than ever to create noise. The danger may not be that AI becomes smarter than humans. The danger may be that humans become less intentional because AI makes everything effortless. When every image can be generated instantly… When every article can be written instantly… When every opinion can be manufactured instantly… The rarest thing becomes authenticity. Ironically, that’s why people continue following the real creators. AI can summarize a factory. It cannot experience one. I actually think that’s where the future advantage lives. Not in fighting AI. That’s why so many users feel exhausted. The algorithm is optimizing for what keeps people scrolling, not necessarily for what they came to see. And when platforms stop respecting user intent, users eventually stop trusting the platform. That’s the real risk. The loss of trust.

I am noticing something almost unsettling on LinkedIn. Something most of us never saw coming. It's the decline of highly formatted, polished content. Let me explain. A few years ago, everyone talked about formatting. The kind where you write one sentence per line, use a lot of white space, and have bullet points everywhere. Basically, the cleaner your post looked, the better. Today, I'm noticing the opposite. Across my own content and the content I create for clients, posts that look too polished often feel less authentic. They feel manufactured, and worse, sometimes they even feel AI-generated. As a result, readers skim the first couple of sentences and move on. The irony? The formatting tactics that once helped content stand out are now making it blend in. I think AI has a lot to do with it. We're surrounded by content that has the same structure, the same rhythm, and the same perfectly spaced formatting. People see it all day long. What readers seem to crave now is something different. -Content that sounds like a real person. -Content that isn't over-edited. -Content that feels conversational, imperfect, and human. I'm not saying formatting doesn't matter. It does. But formatting alone is no longer enough to hold attention or it doesn't make you 'stand out'. So don't bother about it too much (ofcourse don't overcrowd your sentences, lol) What makes you stand out in teh real sense is content that feels raw, genuine and vulnerable.

We can usually tell when you're using AI to write your posts on LinkedIn.

I am not saying you shouldn't use AI for posts on here; I actually think it's smart to use it to tighten up stories and ensure grammar is on point. I just want you to know that people can sometimes tell, and AI can dilute your voice.

There are phrases I've seen over and over again that, at least to me, signal AI right away. So I asked ChatGPT. Here are the phrases it says it uses for LinkedIn content repeatedly:

"And that's important."
"That stuck with me."
"Let that sink in."
"Think about that."
"Read that again."
"It's worth repeating."
"That changed my perspective."
"I'll never forget that."

It also said there are some phrases it is now trying to avoid because they are overused. Tricky little helpful tool, isn't it?

Executive communications is primarily about a person's unique perspective and experience, but voice is important, too. Something to consider as leaders work to create more compelling and authentic content!

"Think about that."

How to Spot AI-Written LinkedIn-Style Posts. Today I took a couple of posts that others had shared, and I ran them through ChatGPT. Unless people are just the perfect writer, editor, etc., there’s a solid chance it was created by AI. Two out of three came in close to 90% possibility that AI generated the majority of the content Full disclosure. The top part was me, the rest of this post comes from ChatGPT. I love how at the end GPT asked if I wanted to write it two different ways to compare. 😊 -------- 1. Structure & Formatting • Predictable arc – AI often follows the same flow: Anecdote → Lesson → Universal takeaway → Call-to-engage questions. • Short, punchy paragraphs – 1–3 lines each, with white space for “scroll appeal.” • Repetition of framing phrases – e.g., “Resilience is not… Resilience is…” or “It’s not about X. It’s about Y.” ⸻ 2. Tone & Language • Polished but generic – Sentences flow smoothly but lack quirks or unique phrasing you’d expect from an individual voice. • Overuse of “inspirational universal” language – Words like resilience, momentum, intentional, growth, goals, leadership, habits appear often. • Perfect grammar & punctuation – Rarely a comma out of place or a conversational “imperfection.” ⸻ 3. Detail Level • Personal details feel “inserted” – Mentions of specific products, places, or events that seem detachable from the main point (e.g., personal item) may have been added post-generation to humanize it. • Lacks messy nuance – No mention of failure that doesn’t resolve neatly, awkward emotions, or unique turns of phrase. AI tends to keep things tidy and uplifting. ⸻ 4. Engagement Triggers • Rhetorical or open-ended ending questions – “What does resilience look like for you?” or “How do you keep going?” • Lists in parallel form – “I trained. I followed up. I wrote.” AI loves rhythmic list structures. ⸻ 5. Editing Tells • Strong flow but uneven authenticity – Often feels “too polished” for a casual personal post, but “too personal” for a corporate memo — that middle ground is where AI + human editing lives. • Tone consistency across different posts – If the author’s tone stays almost exactly the same post after post, it may be templated with AI. ⸻ 💡 Pro tip: AI posts feel “smoothed over” — no jagged edges, slang quirks, or offbeat humor. Human-only writing often has small detours, imperfect rhythm, or surprise turns that AI doesn’t naturally produce without instruction. ⸻ If you’d like, I can rewrite your example post twice — once in a “clear AI” voice and once in a “clear human” voice — so you can see the difference side-by-side. That makes spotting them much easier in the future.
