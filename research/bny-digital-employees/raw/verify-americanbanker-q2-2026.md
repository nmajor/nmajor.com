CEO Robin Vince — who has long been bullish on artificial intelligence — reaffirmed Wednesday his commitment to embedding AI "across the company." During a quarterly earnings call, he told analysts the nation's oldest bank has seen a positive return on its AI investments, with a "tangible and measurable impact across the entire client lifecycle."

Analysts honed in on the BNY's headcount falling 7% in the second quarter, despite revenue continuing to surge.

Chief Financial Officer Dermot McDonogh said the figure was "just an output" and emphasized that the bank continues to "invest heavily in talent."

"It's down 7% over the year, but that's as a result of everything. It's not necessarily a headcount target that we deploy," McDonogh said.

Like other Wall Street heavy hitters, BNY benefited in the second quarter from the market's "unique set of circumstances" and "supportive market backdrop," McDonogh said. He cautioned that year-over-year growth in net interest income and deposits will be difficult to match in the third quarter.

The bank reported $1.45 billion in net interest income last quarter, up 20% from the same period in 2025.
