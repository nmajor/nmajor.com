The best AI failure story I could find was an ad. Here's how I worked that out in 30 seconds.

I wanted one thing: a company that built its own tool with AI, and an honest account of what it cost a year later. Not the launch post. The bill.

The piece everyone forwards is about a custom support tool that replaced a $490-a-month subscription and cost more over six months. It's the only writing anyone points to that puts a number on the part everybody skips.

Every byline on the site belongs to staff at a low-code and AI development agency. The footer on every page advertises that agency. It opens by referring back to a post that doesn't exist.

So the definitive failure story about building it yourself was written by people who sell building it yourself, properly, with them.

The whole investigation, including the success stories I'd cited myself and had to go back and check, is in Actual Intelligence, my newsletter on plain-English AI for people running traditional, non-tech businesses. Link in bio.
