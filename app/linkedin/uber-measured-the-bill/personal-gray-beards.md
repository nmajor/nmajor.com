"Mistakenly we thought that by just introducing artificial intelligence... that would produce a high-quality product." That's a Ford VP.

Charles Poon, Ford's VP of vehicle hardware engineering, was explaining why the company rehired roughly 350 veteran "gray beard" engineers in June, some ex-Ford, some from suppliers, after its AI-run quality control kept missing targets. The same week, on June 25, J.D. Power ranked Ford the No. 1 mainstream brand for initial quality, the first time since 2010. I'm not claiming the rehires produced that result; quality work compounds over years. The two facts still belong in the same paragraph.

Here's the part I'd steal. Ford kept the automation running. The veterans came back to retrain the younger engineers and to reprogram the AI tools, because the memory of how vehicles actually fail in the field turned out to be the training data. Ford had walked it out the door.
