## Editorial choices

The run started with 16 normalized pitches. Astra's evidence pass selected FavTrip, Domtar,
Odyssey and STG beside the required Rockwell companion. The cautionary lane produced Domtar: the
mill already had predictive sensors when a motor failed, then recovered by changing the analyst and
action-follow-through process. Prior cautionary stories about Kinney Drugs and AI coding spend were
rejected as duplicates of published work.

The five posts cover knowledge retrieval, small-business invoice control, predictive maintenance,
legacy software work and logistics order entry. Only FavTrip uses the spike reach game.

## Claims and limits

### Rockwell

- Allowed: technicians had 20 to 30 years' experience; average repair time reportedly moved from
  18 to 12 minutes; the assistant combines captured technician knowledge with manuals and factory
  data; the interface is available beside the equipment.
- Limit: performance figures are company and partner reported without a public measurement method.
- Judgment provenance: `nick-this-week`, based on Nick's stated view that this is both a knowledge
  retrieval problem and an interface story.

### FavTrip

- Allowed: the chain says it reconciles more than 4,550 fuel invoices; the agent progressed from
  reporting to exception flags and then vendor email; Sultan says one wrong-store charge worth
  $25,000 was corrected within minutes.
- Limit: one founder account in trade press, with no independent audit. It is one caught error, not
  an annual saving.
- Judgment provenance: `editorial-proposal`.

### Domtar

- Allowed: a motor failed after the Waites system was already installed; McLaughlin estimated a
  day's sensor data would take 32 weeks to review; the team moved vendor calls from monthly to
  weekly and added other readings; he estimates about 1,547 avoided downtime hours.
- Limit: AI did not cause the motor failure. The downtime number is an employee estimate with no
  published period, cost or audit.
- Judgment provenance: `editorial-proposal`.

### Odyssey

- Allowed: a seven-person Cognizant team moved Access and VBA workflow logic into cloud software,
  used Devin for conversion and supporting engineering work, and required technical-lead or
  architect signoff; suppliers report roughly one-third higher throughput and 37% net cost saving.
- Limit: the evidence is a joint supplier release. It omits the starting budget, comparison method,
  defect rate and human-effort breakdown. The separate 21-day pilot is not used as the platform
  delivery time.
- Judgment provenance: `editorial-proposal`.

### STG

- Allowed: 15 people reportedly spent 60% of their day on roughly five-minute orders with at least
  45 fields; Pallet reports that 95% of the STG orders it processes are touchless.
- Limit: 95% does not mean all STG orders. The deployment predates the September republication, and
  the sources do not publish error, exception-cleanup or redeployment figures.
- Judgment provenance: `editorial-proposal`.

## Audience review

A native Codex juror and a Claude Code Opus juror reviewed the first drafts independently. Persona
simulation is directional and explains little of real audience behavior, so the recurring blockers
were weighted above the scores. Both flagged Odyssey as supplier-coded; the revision added the
actual business workflow, team size and human signoff. A reassessment passed it. Other revisions
made FavTrip's staged authority explicit, narrowed STG's 95% denominator, rounded Domtar's
employee estimate and strengthened Nick's interface judgment in the companion.
