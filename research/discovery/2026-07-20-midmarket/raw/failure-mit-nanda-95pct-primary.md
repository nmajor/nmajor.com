# RAW: MIT NANDA "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" — the "95%" primary, and what it actually measured
Fetched: 2026-07-20
PRIMARY report: MIT Media Lab, Project NANDA (Networked Agents and Decentralized Architecture).
  Published July/August 2025. Lead author: Aditya Challapally. Hosted on the "nandapapers" GitHub repo.
Best methodology write-up found: https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx
Fortune's original coverage (via Yahoo mirror): https://finance.yahoo.com/news/mit-report-95-generative-ai-105412686.html
Legal.io summary: https://www.legal.io/blog/5719519/MIT-Report-Finds-95-of-AI-Pilots-Fail-to-Deliver-ROI-Exposing-GenAI-Divide
Critical read: https://robtlee73.substack.com/p/the-genai-divide-report-a-masterclass

*** USE WITH HEAVY CAVEATS. This is the single most recycled statistic in enterprise AI and it is
    routinely misstated. ***

--- ACTUAL METHODOLOGY, verbatim from Virtualization Review ---
"The report is based on a multi-method research design conducted between January and June 2025.
Researchers performed a systematic review of more than 300 publicly disclosed AI initiatives, held
52 structured interviews with representatives from organizations across industries, and gathered 153
survey responses from senior leaders at four major conferences. Company-specific data and quotations
were anonymized to comply with disclosure policies."

PROBLEMS WITH THAT METHODOLOGY, for our purposes:
1. The survey arm is n=153, recruited AT FOUR CONFERENCES. That is a convenience sample of
   conference attendees, not a random sample of enterprises. No response rate is given.
2. The 300 "initiatives" are PUBLICLY DISCLOSED ones — a sample biased toward things companies chose
   to announce.
3. Company data is anonymised, so nothing in it is independently checkable.
4. It is a Media Lab project report, not a peer-reviewed study.
5. NANDA is itself building agent infrastructure and the report's conclusion is that the fix is
   agentic/learning systems — i.e. the thing the authors work on. Not disinterested.

--- WHAT IT ACTUALLY MEASURED (this is the key misquote) ---
The claim is NOT "95% of AI pilots fail." It is that ~95% of organisations reported NO MEASURABLE
P&L IMPACT, and separately that "only 5% of custom enterprise AI tools reach production."
Verbatim from the report via Virtualization Review:
  "Just 5% of integrated AI pilots are extracting millions in value, while the vast majority remain
   stuck with no measurable P&L impact."
  "only 5% of custom enterprise AI tools reach production."
  "The outcomes are so starkly divided across both buyers (enterprises, mid-market, SMBs) and
   builders (startups, vendors, consultancies) that we call it the GenAI Divide"
"No measurable P&L impact" and "failed" are not the same claim. Most internal tooling of any kind
has never had a measurable P&L line attached to it.

--- THE ONE GENUINELY USEFUL MID-MARKET NUMBER IN IT, verbatim ---
"Enterprises run the most pilots but convert the fewest; mid-market organizations move faster from
pilot to full implementation (~90 days) than large enterprises (nine months or longer)."
*** This is the most quotable mid-market-specific finding in the whole report and it is almost never
    cited, because it cuts against the doom framing. ***

--- OTHER REPORTED FINDINGS ---
- Estimated $30-40B in enterprise GenAI investment over the period.
- >80% of organisations have piloted tools such as ChatGPT or Copilot; ~40% report deployment.
- "Shadow AI": employees in over 90% of firms use personal AI tools even when official pilots fail.
- Practitioner quote from the report: "We've seen dozens of demos this year. Maybe one or two are
  genuinely useful. The rest are wrappers or science projects."
- Claimed back-office case-study savings: "$2-10M in annual savings by replacing outsourced support
  and document review"; "30% reduction in external agency spending"; "$1M annual savings in
  financial risk monitoring." (Anonymised, uncheckable — do not present as verified.)
- Forbes (Jason Snyder, 2025-08-26) reports one specific anonymised case: "At one Fortune 500
  insurer, the sanctioned GenAI pilot looked polished in the boardroom but collapsed in the field
  because it couldn't retain context."

RECOMMENDATION: if Nick cites this at all, cite it as "a Media Lab project report based on 52
interviews and 153 conference-recruited survey responses found that most firms could not attach a
P&L number to their GenAI spend" — never as "MIT found 95% of AI projects fail."
