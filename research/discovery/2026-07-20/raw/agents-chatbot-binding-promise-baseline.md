Core facts (Moffatt v. Air Canada, BC Civil Resolution Tribunal, 14 Feb 2024):
- Jake Moffatt asked Air Canada chatbot about bereavement fares after grandmother died.
- Chatbot invented a policy: book now, apply for retroactive refund within 90 days.
- Actual policy: bereavement discount must be approved BEFORE travel; no post-travel refunds.
- Air Canada refused refund, offered $200 coupon; Moffatt filed CRT claim for CAD $880.
- Air Canada argued "the chatbot is a separate legal entity that is responsible for its own actions."
- Tribunal member Christopher Rivers: "This is a remarkable submission... It should be obvious to Air Canada that it is responsible for all the information on its website. It makes no difference whether the information comes from a static page or a chatbot."
- Found negligent misrepresentation. Awarded CAD $650.88 + interest + tribunal fees (~CAD $812 total).
- Rivers: "I find Air Canada did not take reasonable care to ensure its chatbot was accurate... it does not explain why the webpage titled 'Bereavement travel' was inherently more trustworthy than its chatbot. It also does not explain why customers should have to double-check information found in one part of its website on another part of its website."
- Chatbot was taken off the Air Canada website following the ruling.
- Air Canada CIO Mel Crocker had described the chatbot as an AI "experiment."
