Johnson & Johnson reached nearly 9,000 AI use cases before anyone measured which ones were working.

Its CIO Jim Swanson calls the original approach "a thousand flowers." When they did measure, 10 to 15% of those use cases were producing about 80% of the value.

So they cut to four areas: a co-pilot for sales reps, drug discovery, supply chain risk, and an internal HR chatbot handling around 10 million employee interactions a year.

Nine thousand is the number worth staring at. That's not an experiment budget, it's a portfolio nobody was pruning.

And you can only find the 10% by running long enough to measure it, so the pruning is the skill here rather than the picking.

If someone puts an AI use-case count on a slide in your business, ask how many got switched off this year. A team that can answer is measuring. A team that can't is collecting.
