# The three visual formats

No other social-image format is valid for this project.

Use this fixed selection order: classic meme first, real annotated source screenshot second,
synthetic workbench photo third. The order expresses preference, not permission to force a bad
fit. Move down only when the higher-ranked format cannot communicate the post's actual concept
cleanly or cannot clear its evidence, legibility, safety, or rights gates.

## Classic meme

Use when a known meme relationship carries the argument without explanation. This is the default
format to test first because a clear joke earns attention faster than a proof screenshot.

- Follow `social-meme-campaign` and its active template contracts.
- Preserve the template's low-rent visual character.
- Use the established template image. Never generate an imitation or substitute character scene.
- Use large, short text and the template's established semantic slots.
- Do not add branding or polish.
- Treat template artwork as review-only until its exact rights state is cleared.

## Source capture

Use when no clean meme fits and readers should see the actual webpage, PDF, spreadsheet, product
screen, dashboard, Notes page, or message thread.

- Capture the real rendered source. Never retype it or recreate it with image generation.
- Preserve the source title, domain, page header, table heading, or another origin cue.
- Keep the surrounding sentence, heading, axis, row label, or column label needed to interpret the
  highlighted claim.
- Retain the source's typography, spacing, and natural aspect ratio.
- Prefer one crude circle around the exact claim. Use a box, arrow, or highlight only when a circle
  cannot make the point legible. Keep one annotation color and no more than two marks total.
- One short caption is allowed when the crop cannot retain its origin cue. Use plain black text on
  white or gray.
- Do not add a logo, branded frame, fake browser chrome, rounded card, shadow, texture, or footer.
- Rights remain source-dependent until the exact use clears review.

## Workbench photo

Use only when neither a clear meme nor a real source capture carries the point well. Good subjects
include a used whiteboard, working notebook, scratch calculation, or marked-up generic printout.

- Generate a vertical, mundane phone photo with ordinary light, imperfect framing, and plausible
  physical clutter.
- Give the scene one focal artifact. Do not create a styled flat lay or productivity advertisement.
- Use no more than four short handwritten notes. Source every factual note from the post.
- Keep generic printout body text unreadable. Never fake a real publication, company document,
  product interface, historical photograph, office, meeting, or person.
- Do not add logos, brand colors, digital overlays, decorative grain, or a social-card frame.
- Vary the setting across a batch. Whiteboard, notebook, torn graph paper, and marked-up printout
  should not all share the same props or room.
- Treat it as an illustration. Record the complete prompt and generator. Alt text says it is
  AI-generated or synthetic, and the post must not imply the scene really happened.
- Reject mangled writing, hands, cables, keys, calculators, shadows, reflections, or paper edges.
