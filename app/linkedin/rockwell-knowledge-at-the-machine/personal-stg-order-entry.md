At STG Logistics, 15 people spent 60% of the day turning emailed tenders into orders.

Each order took about five minutes and required at least 45 fields. The incoming files varied, so the work was more than copying one neat form into another.

Pallet says its system now creates 95% of the STG orders it processes without staff keying in the fields. The software reads the incoming material, maps it into the transportation system and sends exceptions to people.

This is a vendor customer story, so the numbers are reported results rather than an independent measurement. It also does not publish the error rate or the work still required on the other 5%.

They automated the messy handoff around the form, which is where the labor was. The published case still leaves the cost of checking exceptions unanswered.
