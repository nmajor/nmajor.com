## WHY THIS IS ON-CONCEPT

The essay: companies report the countable INPUT because the OUTCOME takes a cycle to exist.

This is what happens on the other side of that gap, in court. The claim gets made in a quarter; the correction arrives later; and the gap between them is now the single largest source of alleged investor loss in US securities litigation. AI is 13% of the cases and 73% of the money.

The sharpest detail for a non-tech audience: Cornerstone splits the filings by defendant type, and only 2 of the 15 are "AI Usage" - allegations about a company's claims for AI improving its own existing products (a third filing is coded "AI Development; AI Usage"). The rest are AI developers, infrastructure/hardware and data centres. So the litigation wave is overwhelmingly about companies that SELL AI, not companies that USE it. The exposure for an ordinary operating company is not yet legal. It is internal - a board that believed a number.

## LIMITS / WEAKNESSES
- These are ALLEGATIONS in filed complaints. Nothing here is proven; a filing is not a finding.
- DDL and MDL are market-capitalisation-drop measures, not damages. MDL in particular is a maximum, and two mega-filings drive 66% of it - the headline "$1.35 trillion" is dominated by two cases, almost certainly very large-cap AI names.
- Cornerstone does not name the 15 defendant companies in the extracted spotlight text; the individual companies behind the numbers were NOT verified here.
- The Grundfest quote is truncated in the fetch and must not be used.
- The Figure 2 row-by-row extraction is imperfect for sparse rows; only totals and the "AI Usage" row should be relied on without re-reading the PDF figure.
