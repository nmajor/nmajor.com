I went looking for one honest account of what a homemade AI tool cost a company a year later. The best one I found was written by an agency that sells building them.

Not the launch post. The bill.

The most-quoted cautionary tale in this category is a post about a custom support-triage tool that replaced a $490-a-month Intercom subscription, and whose real six-month cost came out higher than the subscription. It gets shared constantly, and for good reason. It's the only piece of writing anyone points to that puts a number on the part everybody skips.

It's published on a site called Insider AI Trends. Every byline on it belongs to staff at Kreante, a low-code and AI development agency. The footer on every page says "AI app development by Kreante." The About page says the site has "no vendor deals, just an interest in what's working."

The post opens: "Six months ago, we documented what looked like a clean win." There is no such post. The only earlier piece on that site about Intercom quotes completely different numbers, $74 a seat and $15 to $40 a month, and its URL now returns a 404. I searched Hacker News across every tag, no date limit, and got zero results for the domain.

It sits inside a programmatic series with a post for replacing Mixpanel, one for Klaviyo, one for BambooHR. Its sibling post argues that your build method decides "whether the replacement holds for years or breaks in 6 months."

So the definitive failure story about building it yourself was written by people who sell building it yourself, properly, with them.

The numbers in it may well be real. But this is the piece people forward each other as the neutral warning, and it isn't neutral.

Then I went back and checked the success stories. Including two I'd cited myself.

Those didn't hold up either.
