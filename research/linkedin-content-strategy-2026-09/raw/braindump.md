# Research brief: LinkedIn content strategy, September 2026

Captured from Nicholas Major on 2026-09-29.

The current LinkedIn posts have not been getting the reach they used to. Nick suspects the decline began before LinkedIn's explicit AI-slop controls and may have accelerated afterward. He wants current research into:

- how people are successfully using AI to help create or automate LinkedIn content without publishing generic AI-sounding material;
- prompt structures, hook patterns, and content patterns that are still working;
- how to provide real value through unfamiliar, specific stories about people and companies getting value from applied AI;
- whether the current audience is too developer-heavy and how the work could reach more nontechnical leaders and operators;
- whether boosting a few personal posts could widen the audience and produce more relevant followers, and how LinkedIn's paid products actually work;
- what should change in the existing nmajor.com LinkedIn workflow.

Working hypothesis proposed by Nick, to test rather than assume:

- Publish five posts' worth of LinkedIn content each week.
- Only one post needs to relate to that week's newsletter.
- Publish that issue-linked post on Tuesday, roughly one hour after the newsletter.
- Research the other four posts independently within the applied-AI beat, emphasizing concrete stories about people getting value from AI.

No LinkedIn post is approved by this brief. Each eventual post still requires explicit, per-item approval.
