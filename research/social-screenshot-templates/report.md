# Screenshot-native social visuals

Date: 2026-09-23

Scope: Synthesis of the three captures in `raw/`. No new material was fetched. The sources cover Notes-style posts, deliberately low-production ads, and research on why people share screenshots. They do not directly study spreadsheet screenshots, so the spreadsheet guidance below applies the same native-work-surface principles rather than claiming a measured effect.

## Decision

Stop designing evidence cards. Capture the evidence where a person would actually encounter or work with it, then make the smallest useful intervention.

The target is a screenshot someone grabbed because it contained the point. It may come from a browser, PDF, Notes, or a spreadsheet. It should retain enough of that environment to establish where it came from. One crop and one crude annotation are usually enough. A polished composition with fake paper, fake handwriting, tape, shadows, or deliberately crooked elements is still a composition. It reads as an AI image trying to impersonate casual work.

"Shitty" means low-production and recognizably native. It does not mean blurry, unreadable, or stripped of provenance.

## The working templates

### 1. Real source screenshot

Use this when a line in a webpage, report, filing, or PDF is the evidence.

- Capture the real rendered source. Do not retype the passage into a branded frame.
- Keep one origin cue in the crop. Good cues include the publication masthead, report title, author and date, browser address bar, PDF page header, chart title, or table heading.
- Keep one context cue around the evidence. Include the preceding sentence, section heading, axis, row label, or column heading needed to interpret it.
- Crop out navigation and whitespace that do no evidentiary work, but do not crop so tightly that the passage could have come from anywhere.
- Add at most one highlight, circle, underline, box, or arrow. It should point to the exact claim, not decorate the whole image.
- Put the direct source URL in the post or ledger even when the screenshot shows the site name. A screenshot is a fixed view, not proof that the underlying page has not changed.

The screenshot-sharing paper calls screenshots a portable form of evidence, but also a "digital window" that hides the live system and can remove context. It documents highlighting, circles, arrows, cropping, redaction, and stitched screenshots as ordinary ways people guide interpretation. That makes simple annotation native to the medium. It also makes retained context and a retrievable source non-negotiable. [Raw paper](raw/why-people-share-screenshots.pdf), [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27539)

### 2. Actual Notes screenshot

Use this for a short personal observation, rough list, or calculation that could plausibly have been written in Notes before it became a post.

- Make the note in the actual app and capture it. A faithful export tool is second choice. A custom recreation inside the renderer is not a screenshot.
- Leave ordinary app structure visible, such as the back label, first-line title treatment, timestamp, toolbar, or uneven bottom crop. Do not display every control merely to prove it is Notes.
- Use the system font and default spacing. Do not substitute a handwriting font.
- Let the words make ragged line endings. Use short blocks and blank lines. Do not force the text into a centered poster shape.
- Front-load the point because feed crops preserve the top of the note. If the text becomes small at phone width, cut it or use a second screenshot.
- Do not place the Notes capture on a colored brand card, add a logo footer, or frame it inside a pristine phone mockup. Those moves turn a note back into an ad.

The Notes capture argues that the format feels personal because the page does not announce a set of design decisions. The first line becomes a heavier title, blank lines preserve readability, and decoration weakens the effect. The source itself is a vendor guide, not controlled research, so use it as a description of the format rather than performance proof. [Raw capture](raw/iphone-notes-aesthetic.html), [source page](https://javii.tools/blog/iphone-notes-aesthetic)

### 3. Actual spreadsheet screenshot

Use this for arithmetic, a small comparison, a scoring pass, or evidence assembled during analysis.

- Build the calculation in a real spreadsheet and capture the working area.
- Keep row numbers, column letters, gridlines, a selected cell, formula bar, or sheet tab when they help prove this is working material.
- Show inputs and the formula or output together. A lone total in one large cell is just a KPI tile wearing spreadsheet clothes.
- Use plain labels a person would type while working. Keep normal column widths, clipped secondary text, sparse empty cells, and a practical amount of visible sheet around the useful range.
- Basic fills, bold headers, currency formats, and one total row are fine. Avoid a coordinated palette, merged title banner, decorative chart, rounded overlays, or a tidy dashboard region.
- Crop to the small relevant range. Do not zoom so far out that the cells turn into texture.

This is an application of the sources rather than a finding about spreadsheets specifically. The useful property is the visible native work surface. The Selfstorming capture describes the low-production pattern as plain text, a screenshot, or a phone photo that looks like an ordinary post. [Raw capture](raw/selfstorming-ugly-ad.html), [source page](https://www.selfstorming.com/tools/libraries/social-media-ad-techniques/formats/ugly-ad)

### 4. Screenshot strip or contact sheet

Use this when two to four captures together establish chronology, contradiction, or comparison.

- Keep each capture rectangular. Stack them with narrow, plain gaps rather than placing them in rounded cards.
- Preserve the identifying cue on every source, not only the first one.
- Use one handwritten number or short label per capture only when sequence is unclear.
- Do not normalize every crop to the same height if that removes context. A slightly awkward stack looks more truthful because the source material really has different shapes.
- Never stitch unrelated snippets so proximity implies a relationship the sources do not support.

The screenshot paper describes aggregation as a way to assemble several posts into one argument or narrative. That same section warns, by implication and through its broader discussion, that the person making the composite controls the frame. [Raw paper](raw/why-people-share-screenshots.pdf), [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27539)

## Annotation and crop rules

The annotation should look like an action taken on a screenshot, not a second design layer.

- Prefer the operating system's markup tool or an equally plain equivalent.
- Use one color. Red or yellow is enough.
- Draw one imperfect stroke. Do not manufacture wobble, vary line weight theatrically, or add a handwritten essay in the margin.
- Keep the mark off the letters it is meant to expose.
- Redact private material with a solid block. Do not blur it and hope the text cannot be recovered.
- Retain the source name plus the local page context needed to read the claim correctly.
- When a crop changes the apparent meaning, widen it. When no crop can preserve the qualification, do not use the screenshot.
- Label a reconstruction on the image. Never make a fake interface or invented document look like a capture.

## What still smells generated

Reject the visual when the roughness has been art-directed. Common tells:

- fake masking tape, paper grain, coffee rings, torn edges, folded corners, or desktop props;
- handwriting fonts and perfectly spaced "scribbles";
- a source excerpt retyped into a simulated browser, Notes window, terminal, or spreadsheet;
- pristine symmetry underneath a few rotated elements;
- identical margins, shadows, annotation colors, and crop ratios across a batch;
- a brand palette hiding inside every supposedly casual artifact;
- large empty areas arranged around one metric;
- rounded containers around screenshot fragments;
- a fake cursor, fake selection, fake formula bar, or fake mobile chrome;
- every important object conveniently visible, aligned, and unobstructed;
- deliberate typos, fake timestamps, or random redactions added as authenticity props;
- high-resolution texture and cinematic lighting applied to ordinary office material.

The core tell is coherence. Real screenshots inherit awkwardness from the source application and the moment of capture. Faux-rough designs distribute imperfection evenly because someone designed the imperfection.

## Skill-ready rule set

For each evidence-led visual:

1. Capture the real browser, PDF, Notes page, or spreadsheet.
2. Keep one provenance cue and one context cue.
3. Crop to the smallest region that remains honest.
4. Add no more than one annotation unless several screenshots need sequence labels.
5. Test at about 300 pixels wide. Cut content before shrinking type.
6. Record the direct URL, capture date, local raw source, rights state, and alt text in the ledger.
7. Reject the asset if its "human" quality comes from decorative distress rather than the native source.

The renderer should support cropping, padding, stacking, a plain background, redaction, and simple markup. It should not recreate apps or supply an "ugly" skin. If the raw evidence is unavailable as a genuine capture, use a clearly labeled reconstruction or choose another visual mode.
