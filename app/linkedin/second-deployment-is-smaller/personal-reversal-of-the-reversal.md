You may remember the bank that replaced a bunch of people with AI, then had to give them their jobs back.

Almost nobody knows what happened a year later.

August 2025: Commonwealth Bank cut 45 direct banking roles, saying a new voice bot had reduced call volumes by 2,000 a week. The union said volumes were actually rising, and that team leaders were being pulled onto the phones to cope. The bank admitted its assessment "did not adequately consider all relevant business considerations", and hired people for those roles again.

What is less talked about is that while this was happening a completely separate system was quietly getting good. Hey CommBank, its chat assistant, launched in late 2024, and by May this year it was resolving almost nine in ten customer conversations on its own.

Then in July the job losses arrived, well away from where the headlines had been. Hundreds of roles went at Nutun, the Johannesburg contractor that had been running CBA's online chat.

Those 45 kept their jobs because they were CBA staff, covered by an agreement, with a union that took the bank to a tribunal until it backed down. The Nutun contractors had none of that, and were never on CBA's headcount to begin with.

So the cost came out in the end. It came out of the outsourced tier, and it came once the AI was aimed at a job it could actually do.
