## Key claims (WebSearch synthesis — VERIFY EACH BEFORE USE)

### UK CMA — March 2026
"In March 2026, the CMA told UK businesses that the same consumer law applies whether a customer deals with a human or an AI agent, and that the business is responsible even when a third party supplied or designed the agent."
=> This is the UK regulator stating the Air Canada principle as prospective guidance. HIGH VALUE — find the primary CMA publication.

### Salesforce Agentic Order Management — June 2026
"In June 2026, Salesforce launched Agentic Order Management, a system that lets retailers control fulfillment through plain-language instructions, allowing logistics managers to direct AI to make decisions like 'ship from the closest warehouse with available stock' across 7,000 locations and 250 million inventory records."
"When an AI agent routes an order to the wrong warehouse, misreads available inventory or trades delivery reliability for a margin gain, the processor records a clean transaction, but the merchant gets a dispute with no easy explanation because the decision happened in a system with no audit trail the payments chain can read."

### Clifford Chance — the contractual liability gap
"Many technology agreements exclude liability for exactly the types of harm that defective agentic AI is most likely to cause, meaning if an AI agent incorrectly authorizes a supplier payment, misprices a product, or issues misleading communications, the supplier's disclaimers often absolve them of responsibility."
"Scale the liability logic to a $50,000 enterprise software contract or a bulk inventory order and the liability question becomes very serious very quickly."

### American Express
The Financial Brand: Amex has published a partial answer on who bears loss when an AI agent makes an incorrect purchase. NAMED, established financial institution setting agentic-commerce liability policy proactively. Worth a direct read.
