## Pitch 1 — They already built this foundation once

**Hook (<=140 chars):**
In 2019, eight companies formed a foundation because cloud bills broke their budgets. In June, the same people announced one for AI tokens.

**Story (3-4 sentences):**
In February 2019, after a run of cloud bill-shock stories — including Adobe's dev team burning ~$80,000 a day on Azure for over a
week before anyone noticed, north of half a million dollars — eight companies including Atlassian and Spotify formed the FinOps
Foundation, which the Linux Foundation absorbed in 2020 and which grew into a standard corporate discipline. On June 9, 2026, at
FinOps X, the Linux Foundation and FinOps Foundation announced intent to form the Tokenomics Foundation — "a vendor-neutral home"
for standardizing AI billing, backed by Oracle, Google, Microsoft, IBM, SAP, JPMorganChase and more. The trigger data is the same
shape as 2018's: per the State of FinOps 2026 survey, 98% of FinOps teams now manage AI spend (31% two years ago), 73% of
companies blew their AI cost plans, and agentic projects overshoot budgets 2.4x. The cloud version took six years to go from bill
shock to mature discipline; the precedent says token cost management becomes a job title, a tooling market, and a billing
standard — and that spending caps are the crude first stage every company passes through on the way there.

  report at https://data.finops.org/ ; 73% / 2.4x recapped at https://www.usage.ai/blogs/finops/ai-ml-cost/finops-x-2026-takeaways .

**Why now:** The announcement is eight weeks old and 2027 budget planning starts this fall — readers are about to set their first
AI line items with actual institutional guidance available. And this week's issue put "you can measure the bill but not the work"
in front of Nick's audience; this is the what-happens-next chapter without re-treading Uber.

**First-person angle:** Nick built through the cloud-bill era — surprise invoices, tagging sweeps, rightsizing meetings — and can
honestly write "I have watched this exact movie before, and here is which scenes come next." He can also carry the skeptical
kicker the foundations never say out loud: FinOps got very good at answering "what does the cloud cost" and never answered "was
it worth it" — the same input-vs-output gap the COO class is complaining about now (his essay's theme, extended forward, not
repeated).

**Breakout potential:** High. "History repeating, with dates" travels on reshares; the 31%->98%-in-two-years stat and the
2019/2026 mirror give a stranger a one-line reason to share ("we are speed-running the FinOps era"). Named backers (Microsoft,
JPMorgan, SAP) make it feel like news, not opinion.

## Pitch 2 — ChatGPT is on the expense report, right where Dropbox used to sit

**Hook (<=140 chars):**
ChatGPT is now the most-expensed app in corporate America. The last software that got in that way took companies a decade to claw back.

**Story (3-4 sentences):**
SaaS entered companies bottom-up — employees expensing Dropbox and Slack, departments buying on cards — until portfolios passed
300 apps in 2021 and the bill forced a rationalization wave: Zylo's 2024 index shows the average portfolio cut from 291 to 269
apps with an average of $18M a year still wasted on unused licenses. AI apps are re-running the entry half of that curve faster:
Zylo's 2026 index has ChatGPT as the most-expensed application, expensed software spend up 267% in a year, and business units —
not IT — controlling 81% of software spend; and the fresh 90-day data says governance is losing, with a May 30 Cloud Security
Alliance note finding eight in ten employees use unapproved AI tools (invisible to security 89% of the time, undetected for a
median of 403 days) and CIO.com reporting in late June that 77% of tech leaders say AI adoption is outpacing their governance.
The precedent predicts the second half of the curve: an AI-app rationalization wave inside about two years, a discovery-and-
cost-management tooling market (already starting — Zylo shipped an AI consumption-cost product in April), and renewal-time
shock, since per Gartner much of the AI cost is arriving hidden inside existing vendors' renewals at ~30% uplifts. The twist
that makes it worth writing: seat sprawl wasted money idly, but AI sprawl is usage-priced and agentic — the waste compounds
per-call, and the cleanup will be uglier than cancelling forgotten seats.

**Verified sources:**
- Precedent: Zylo 2024 SaaS Management Index (portfolios 291->269, -17% vs 2021, $18M unused-license waste) —
  https://zylo.com/news/2024-saas-management-index/ and https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/2024-saas-management-index-reveals-an-average-of-18m-in-annual-license-waste-with-significant-security-risks-from-employee-expensed-apps-302071679.html ;
  Productiv consolidation data (374->342 apps) — https://productiv.com/blog/it-saas-statistics/ ; chiefmartec (Feb 2024) on
  stacks shrinking 8% — https://chiefmartec.com/2024/02/well-saas-tech-stacks-shrank-from-2023-to-2024-but-only-by-8-you-were-expecting-more/ .
- Backdrop data: Zylo 2026 SaaS Management Index, Jan 29, 2026 (fetched direct: ChatGPT most-expensed; +267% expensed spend;
  78% unexpected AI/consumption charges; 61% cut projects; BUs control 81% of spend; 218 IT leaders + 40M licenses/$75B) —
  https://zylo.com/news/2026-saas-management-index ; Zylo consumption-cost product launch, Apr 14, 2026 —
  https://zylo.com/news/ai-consumption-cost-management-launch .
- 90-day peg: CSA research note, May 30, 2026 (8-in-10 unapproved AI use; 89% invisible; 403-day median detection) —
  https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-shadow-ai-apps-enterprise-20260530-csa-sty/ (fetched);
  CIO.com, June 30, 2026, Stephanie Overby (IBM 2026 Tech Leader Study 77%; Protiviti: ~2/3 of companies see unsupervised AI
  use, ~half of large enterprises lack visibility; Gartner ~30% undisclosed AI renewal uplift) —
  https://www.cio.com/article/4189671/beware-ai-costs-hidden-in-plain-sight.html (fetched).

**Why now:** Both peg pieces are from the last nine weeks, fall renewal season is when the hidden 30% uplifts land, and the
"invisible worker on the expense report" is the exact blind spot a mid-market operator can act on this quarter — count the AI
apps before the renewal does it for you.

**First-person angle:** Nick was the sprawl. As a builder he expensed the tools that became shadow IT — signed up with a company
card, got value, created the line item nobody owned — and later sat through the rationalization sweeps. "I was the guy your IT
department was hunting" is an honest, disarming frame no analyst report can carry, and it lets him say what the employee side
knows: the tools got expensed because they worked, which is why bans fail and visibility wins.

**Breakout potential:** High. "ChatGPT is the most-expensed app in corporate America" is a stat strangers reshare on its own;
pairing it with the Dropbox-era memory gives every operator over 35 a knowing-nod share, and the 403-days-undetected number is
the screenshot line. Risk: shadow-AI security angles are common — the differentiator is the precedent's cost curve (sprawl ->
$18M waste -> forced rationalization), which almost nobody pairs with the 2026 data.
