## https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2024/11/22/327550.htm
Class Action Lawsuit on AI-Related Discrimination Reaches Final Settlement.
Louis' attorneys + DOJ statement of interest argued SafeRent's algorithm could be held accountable because it still plays a role in access to housing. Judge denied SafeRent's motion to dismiss on those counts.
Settlement stipulates SafeRent can't include its score feature on tenant screening reports in certain cases, including if the applicant is using a housing voucher. Requires that any new screening score be validated by a third party plaintiffs agree to.
Louis' son found an affordable apartment on Facebook Marketplace, $200 more expensive, less desirable area.
Suit accused algorithm of relying too much on credit information; fails to give full picture of ability to pay rent; unfairly dings applicants with housing vouchers who are Black and Hispanic partly because of lower median credit scores from historical inequities.
Todd Kaplan (plaintiff attorney): "Management companies and landlords need to know that they're now on notice, that these systems that they are assuming are reliable and good are going to be challenged."
Christine Webber: data an algorithm uses or weights could have "the same effect as if you told it to discriminate intentionally."

## https://finance.yahoo.com/news/saferent-settles-2-3m-discrimination-152813034.html
SafeRent Settles $2.3M Discrimination Lawsuit Over Alleged AI Screening Bias Against Low-Income Renters.
US District Judge Angel Kelley granted final approval of ~$2.3 million settlement. Lawsuit filed in Massachusetts. Accused SafeRent's algorithm of disproportionately scoring Black and Hispanic tenants, and those using housing vouchers, lower.
SafeRent will stop using AI-generated scores to evaluate applicants who use housing vouchers; will cease providing accept/deny recommendations for voucher applicants.

## https://civilrights.org/resource/ai-tenant-screening
AI + Tenant Screening (Leadership Conference).
SafeRent Solutions settled a $2.275 million lawsuit; plaintiffs alleged the AI system failed to account for applicants' housing vouchers, automatically denying them based on ability to pay without the voucher. One of the 400 plaintiffs, Mary Louis, was denied despite a recommendation from her previous landlord of 17 years, a housing voucher, and a cosigner's high credit score.
Cites: Bains, "When Machines Discriminate: The Critical Role of Disparate Impact in AI Accountability," Leadership Conference Center for Civil Rights and Technology, January 22, 2026.
Cites: DOJ "Rescinding Portions of Department of Justice Title VI Regulations To Conform More Closely With the Statutory Text and To Implement Executive Order 14281," Federal Register, December 10, 2025.
