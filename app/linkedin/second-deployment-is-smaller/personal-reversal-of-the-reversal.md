Everyone shares the story of the bank that reversed its AI job cuts. Almost nobody shares what happened next.

August 2025: Commonwealth Bank cut 45 contact-centre roles, saying its voice bot had reduced call volumes. The union said volumes were actually rising. The bank admitted an error and reinstated the roles, and the story went round the world as proof the pendulum swings back.

July 2026: the narrowed chat assistant is resolving close to nine in ten conversations, and the bank has cut hundreds of chat roles, moving that work to an outsourcer in Johannesburg. That second act comes from the Australian Computer Society's Information Age, and as far as I can tell it's the only outlet that's run it, so weigh it accordingly.

The over-scoped version got rolled back loudly. The right-scoped one got kept quietly. Only the first kind makes the news, which is why a lot of people's mental model of how this goes is built entirely on the first act.

So if you're citing that reversal as evidence the work comes back, check what the same company did a year later. The routine tier went anyway, and what's left is the harder calls, staffed by whoever was cheapest.
