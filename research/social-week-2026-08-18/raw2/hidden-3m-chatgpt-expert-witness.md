## Verbatim / documented

From 404 Media (free portion):
> Expert witness asked ChatGPT to "create an exceptional expert witness report defending the standard of care at 3M" and to "show how 3M is 0% at fault for the explosion"
> $61 million lawsuit involving a 2020 Houston explosion at Watson Grinding that killed three people and destroyed approximately 200 homes
> Root cause: a "degraded and poorly crimped rubber welding hose" that leaked flammable gas
> Homeowners sued 3M and Watson Grinding, claiming 3M failed to properly service the facility's gas detection system

From Futurism:
> Expert witness: Josh Autenrieth of Knighthawk Engineering
> Prompts: "create an exceptional expert witness report defending the standard of care at 3M" and "show how 3M is 0 percent at fault"
> Report length: 30 pages
> 350 pages of ChatGPT activity logs were obtained
> Attorney commentary from plaintiffs' legal team; he "exclusively relied on ChatGPT to form his opinions"

From reporter's summary thread (Jason Koebler, 404 Media):
> "An 'expert witness' in a $61 million lawsuit over an industrial explosion that killed three people and destroyed 200 homes used ChatGPT to write his report for the court. He prompted ChatGPT to 'show how 3M is 0% at fault for the explosion at Watson Grinding'"

## Why it matters to a non-technical executive
A vendor your company paid for expert judgement can now be delivering ChatGPT output under their own letterhead — and discovery will surface the prompt log. The reputational hit lands on the company that hired them, not the vendor.
