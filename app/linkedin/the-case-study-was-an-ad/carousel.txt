## Slide 1 — hook

**5 checks before you believe an AI case study**

I fact-checked the ones I'd cited myself.
Two didn't survive it.

---

## Slide 2 — check 1

**1. Find the publisher, not the author**

Read the About page and the footer first.

The most-cited cautionary tale about building
your own software is published by a development
agency. Every byline is its own staff.

Not false. Marketing. Read it differently.

---

## Slide 3 — check 2

**2. Follow the number backwards**

One district's saving was reported as:

$250,000 in April
$220,000 in May
$250,000 again by late May

An earlier interview has no figure at all.

Each retelling cites the last one.

---

## Slide 4 — check 3

**3. Ask for the denominator**

The $250,000 saving was real.

The same board approved a $4.8m
hardware lease over the same period.

The saving is ~4.6% of one contract.

A number without its budget isn't a result.

---

## Slide 5 — check 4

**4. Look for year two**

Nearly every build-your-own success story
reports the first year.

I could not find one that published the second.

The maintenance reckoning everyone warns about
is asserted constantly and evidenced almost nowhere.

---

## Slide 6 — check 5

**5. Ask what forced it into the open**

Every failure I could verify surfaced because
something compelled it:

A parliamentary committee
Congressional testimony
A union arbitration
A regulator's disclosure deadline

No committee, no regulator, no union?
Nothing makes a company publish the bad year.

---

## Slide 7 — recap

**The five checks**

1. Who published it
2. Does the number drift
3. What's the denominator
4. Is there a year two
5. What forced it into the open

The published record isn't a sample of what happens.
It's a sample of what someone was forced to say.
