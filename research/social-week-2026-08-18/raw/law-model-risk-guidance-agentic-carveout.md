## SOURCE 1 — OCC Bulletin 2026-13 (PRIMARY, regulator)
URL: https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-13.html
Publication: Office of the Comptroller of the Currency
Title: "Model Risk Management: Revised Guidance"
Date published/issued: 2026-04-17
Date accessed: 2026-08-18

Verbatim / extracted:

- Applies to: "Chief Executive Officers of All National Banks, Federal Savings Associations,
  and Federal Branches and Agencies; Department and Division Heads; All Examining Personnel;
  and Other Interested Parties"
- Model definition: a model is "a complex quantitative method, system, or approach that applies
  statistical, economic, or financial theories to process input data into quantitative
  estimates." The definition explicitly excludes simple arithmetic calculations and rule-based
  processes lacking statistical or economic foundations.
- KEY LINE: "Generative AI and agentic AI models are novel and rapidly evolving. As such, they
  are not within the scope of this guidance."
- Forthcoming: the agencies "plan to issue in the near future a request for information that
  addresses model risk management generally and considers, in particular, banks' use of AI,
  including generative AI and agentic AI and AI-based models."

## SOURCE 3 — Sullivan & Cromwell LLP client memo
URL: https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/April/OCC-Fed-FDIC-Issue-Revised-Guidance-Model-Risk-Management
Publication: Sullivan & Cromwell LLP
Title: "Federal Banking Agencies Issue Revised Guidance on Model Risk Management"
Date published: 2026-04-29
Date accessed: 2026-08-18

Verbatim / extracted — key changes from SR 11-7:

1. Narrower model definition — explicitly excludes "simple arithmetic calculations, such as
   those found within spreadsheets"
2. AI exclusion — generative and agentic AI models are excluded because these technologies are
   "novel and rapidly evolving"
3. Materiality framework — model materiality depends on purpose and exposure; organizations may
   determine certain models immaterial
4. Principles-based approach — removes prescriptive requirements like mandatory annual
   validation cycles
5. Enforceability disclaimer — the guidance "does not set forth enforceable standards" and
   "non-compliance with this guidance will not result in supervisory criticism"
6. Vendor model validation — streamlined expectations, no longer explicitly requiring
   contingency plans

- $30 billion threshold: guidance is "expected to be most relevant to banking organizations with
  over $30 billion in total assets," though smaller institutions with significant model risk
  exposure remain covered.
- Planned RFI: the agencies "plan to issue in the near future a request for information that
  addresses model risk management generally and considers, in particular, banks' use of AI,
  including generative AI and agentic AI and AI-based models."
