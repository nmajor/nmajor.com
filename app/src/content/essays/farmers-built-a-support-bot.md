Farmers Insurance is touring a 16.4 million-hour AI success story built around a support bot.

The figure comes from Phil Leininger, a Farmers executive, in an [Insurance Business interview](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/how-farmers-used-ai-to-free-up-16-4-million-agent-hours-587592.aspx). In June 2024, Farmers started a two-year program called Agency Servicing Efficiency 35. The goal was to cut the routine servicing work done by more than 8,000 agents and roughly 20,000 staff by 35%.

Leininger says the program reached that target, freeing 16.4 million hours a year. Farmers repeated both numbers publicly.

The AI product Leininger described is askfarmers.ai. It put more than 300,000 internal documents, notifications, and articles behind one conversational search box. Those materials had been spread across five systems covering policies, procedures, rates, forms, and company updates.

Farmers says the bot now resolves more than 60% of questions that previously became calls to a service center.

That sounds useful. It also sounds like a support bot.

## The number belongs to a much bigger program

Farmers has not shown how much of the 16.4 million hours came from askfarmers.ai. Its own account credits process simplification and AI tools together. The company has not published query volumes, adoption, accuracy, escalation rates, or a calculation connecting the bot to the headline.

The wider program involved office visits, forums, and listening sessions with agents. Farmers started with its highest-volume personal insurance lines, then involved underwriting, product, distribution, agency partners, sales, and back-office teams.

That is a two-year operating overhaul. The search assistant was one piece of it.

The measurement is hard to inspect. Leininger described a baseline of "close to 50 million hours" a year. Thirty-five percent of exactly 50 million is 17.5 million, while 16.4 million implies a baseline closer to 46.9 million. The figures can reconcile through rounding, but Farmers has not published the underlying calculation.

The 16.4 million hours also represent estimated time returned to agents. Farmers disclosed no wage assumption, realized cost reduction, extra revenue, or independent audit.

## Boring support bots can still pay off

Farmers is not the only company reporting time savings from internal search.

[FletcherTech](https://cloud.google.com/customers/fletchertech) tested Gemini Enterprise with 222 employees for three months. Google reports 31,778 answers and more than 2,500 hours saved, although the tool also handled content generation, analysis, and custom agents.

[IBM](https://www.ibm.com/case-studies/ibm-software-support) says its support assistant saves about 10 minutes when it produces a correct answer. IBM projects roughly 17,000 hours saved annually, but reports current answer accuracy of 28%.

[Elastic](https://www.elastic.co/resources/article/elastic-elasticgpt) says its Slack-based internal assistant saves more than five hours per employee each month and paid for itself within two months. The company does not disclose how it calculated the time, and its own deployment avoided the licensing costs a normal customer would pay.

Carlsberg and Orion Health have published similar stories. Their results range from a small test that cut a 30-minute search to seconds to an expected 50 support hours saved each day. Most of these figures come from the companies selling or using the tools, with limited measurement details. They show that meaningful savings are possible. They do not show how often these projects work.

A 2026 [INSEAD field experiment](https://ssrn.com/abstract=6028034) offers stronger evidence. Researchers randomized 42 teams at one technology services company. After three months, employees with a grounded internal assistant reported better access to knowledge and worked across more projects than the control group. The tool did more than search, the outcomes were mostly self-reported, and the study covered one company.

A support bot can earn a real return without remaking an industry. Askfarmers.ai is one search box over 300,000 scattered documents that Farmers says keeps most former agent questions away from its phone queue. That is a useful tool. The 16.4 million-hour story is doing much more work than the evidence.
