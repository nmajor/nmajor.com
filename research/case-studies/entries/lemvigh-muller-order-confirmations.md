## What they did
The Danish wholesaler put SAP AI agents onto supplier order confirmations — the routine work of
matching an incoming supplier confirmation against the purchase order and flagging the
discrepancies — inside its existing procurement process, aiming at "touchless" handling. A
follow-up piece in August extends the same approach to broader document processing.

## The numbers
- Target volume: ~100,000 manual order confirmations per year moved to agents (vendor-reported).
- No published before/after on cycle time, error rate, cost, or headcount.

## What broke / the caveats
Entirely vendor-published (SAP News and an SAP asset), so this is marketing with a named
customer attached. The 100,000 figure is the scope of the work, not a measured result: there is
no disclosed accuracy rate, no exception-handling rate, and nothing on what happens when the
agent gets a match wrong on a steel order. Treat as a deployment sighting rather than a proven
outcome until an independent or company-side account appears.

## Why it matters for our beat
Order confirmation matching is the archetypal boring, high-volume, rules-adjacent back-office
job in a 180-year-old distribution business — exactly the shape of work most traditional
companies actually have. The corroborating signal is that Kloeckner & Co (steel distribution,
founded 1906) has a near-identical PO confirmation automation case published separately by a
different vendor. Two of the same story in the same industry within months says the pattern is
real even if each individual number is vendor-supplied.
