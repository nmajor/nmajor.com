# Blind newsletter drafting prompt

Write one complete draft of an issue of **Actual Intelligence, by Nicholas Major** about
Farmers Insurance's Agency Servicing Efficiency 35 program.

Use only the attached research report for factual claims. Treat every result attributed
to Farmers or Phil Leininger as company-reported, not independently audited. Do not add
facts from memory or web search. Preserve source links inline at the claims they support.

The article should explain what Farmers did, what it changed, and why the way it approached
the work produced such a large reported impact. The editorial position is that Farmers did
the hard research before applying AI. It asked agents and their staff which routine tasks
were consuming time without adding value, studied the work, simplified it, and then built
tools around the problems it had found.

Do not convert that observation into advice. Do not tell the reader what to do, ask the
reader a diagnostic question, announce "the lesson," or use a "do this, not that" close.
Let the evidence carry the implication. End with a direct observation in this register:
this is a strong example of the depth of research required to identify where AI can
actually make a difference. Do not copy that sentence mechanically if a plainer ending
fits the draft better.

Follow the attached nmajor.com writing-voice skill, Nicholas Major voice profile,
blacklist, and unslop skill. Where they conflict, follow the writing-voice skill and the
editorial directions in this prompt.

Requirements:

- 500 to 700 words, excluding the title and summary.
- Concise, punchy, and information-dense.
- Start on the strongest concrete fact. No wind-up.
- Include a title and a one-sentence summary, then the essay body.
- Use first person only for an honest editorial judgment. Do not invent personal
  experience, conversations, or work Nicholas did.
- No headings unless the argument genuinely needs one.
- No em dashes.
- No calls to action or commercial pitch.
- No postscript explaining your choices.

Before returning the draft, silently run the writing-voice audit and the unslop audit.
Return only the title, summary, and finished essay.
