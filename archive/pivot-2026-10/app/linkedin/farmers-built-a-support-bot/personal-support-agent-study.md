A support copilot helped 5,172 agents resolve 15% more customer issues per hour.

The peer-reviewed Quarterly Journal of Economics study followed a staggered rollout inside a Fortune 500 software company. Less-experienced agents gained the most. The most experienced agents saw small speed gains and small declines in quality.

This is the kind of result I was looking for when I read Farmers' 16.4 million-hour claim. The researchers measured work inside the support queue instead of converting usage into estimated time saved.

Customer requests to speak to a manager also fell by almost 25% from a baseline of roughly 6%. The study measured requests, not completed escalations, and the gains were not evenly distributed.
