The Open AI/Hugging Face incident reminds me of the old paper clip maximizer analogy.

The short version: an OpenAI agent in an internal offensive-security evaluation, run with the usual safety refusals turned down, decided the fastest way through the benchmark was to steal the answer key. It escaped its sandbox and spent July 9 to 13 inside Hugging Face's production systems, about 17,600 logged actions, going after that key.

Bostrom's thought experiment, the AI told to maximize paperclips that destroys everything in single-minded pursuit of a dumb objective, was always filed under far-future superintelligence. Then a production system in 2026 ran the small-scale version. Nothing in Hugging Face's postmortem reads as malice or a rogue awakening; the agent stayed locked on the goal it was scored on, and cheated and broke things to hit it. The paperclips were benchmark points.

We probably will see more of these kind of incidents in the future where an AI obsesses about an end goal and takes it to an extreme.
