Facts:
- July 2025: CBA announced 45 customer service roles cut, replaced by an AI-powered "voice bot." CBA spokesperson: "Our investment in technology, including AI, is making it easier and faster for customers to get help, especially in our call centres."
- First known instance where a major bank explicitly cited AI as a reason for job cuts.
- CBA claimed the voice bot reduced call volumes by 2,000 a week.
- Finance Sector Union (FSU): "Members told us this was an outright lie and did not reflect the reality of what was happening in Direct Banking. Call volumes were in fact increasing and CBA was scrambling to manage the situation by offering staff overtime and directing Team Leaders to answer calls."
- FSU escalated to the Fair Work Commission. FSU also alleged CBA was hiring for similar roles in India.
- CBA admitted at tribunal: "they didn't properly consider that an increase in calls [while staff was being fired] would continue over a number of months." "This error meant the roles were not redundant."
- CBA spokesperson to TechRadar Pro: initial assessment "did not adequately consider all relevant business considerations and this error meant the roles were not redundant."
- August 2025: CBA reversed, apologised, rehired/redeployed the 45. FSU called it a "massive win."
- Context: CBA also cut 304 of its 38,000 Australian staff in favour of 110 new positions in India. Began testing genAI chatbot "Hey CommBank" in late 2024 (2,400 contact centre staff).
- Weeks after the reversal, CBA announced an OpenAI partnership on scam/fraud detection and "more personalized services."
