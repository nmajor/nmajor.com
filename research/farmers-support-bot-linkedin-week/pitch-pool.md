# Pitch pool for the remaining Farmers support-bot week

Goal: choose four posts for Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, and the following Monday. Tuesday's
approved Farmers meme post is already published. The week should build trust in Nicholas
Major's practical, skeptical AI judgment and earn relevant follows, saves, or substantive
comments. Most posts should play the narrow save game. At most one should be a breakout-shaped
story, and only if its peg is strong enough. No hard sell.

Score every pitch for stop-scroll appeal, relevance to an AI decision-maker at a traditional
company, credibility/evidence quality, and breakout potential. A clever hook cannot rescue weak
evidence. Pick four distinct ideas rather than four versions of "the savings number is shaky."

## E0. Farmers' number belongs to the overhaul

Farmers says a two-year program freed 16.4 million hours. The only concrete AI product it has
described is askfarmers.ai, but its own account credits process simplification and cross-company
work alongside AI. Angle: a useful support bot has been made to carry a much larger operating
story. Source: https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/technology/how-farmers-used-ai-to-free-up-16-4-million-agent-hours-587592.aspx

## M1. Google counts silence as search success

Gemini Enterprise counts an answer as successful when the user gives it a thumbs-up or does not
ask another question within 30 seconds. Google then assigns three minutes saved to each success
and multiplies by a customer-supplied hourly wage. Angle: this is how product activity becomes
an executive "value saved" number. Source: https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/view-analytics

## M2. Microsoft's Worten arithmetic misses by 6,625 hours

Microsoft says more than 75,000 annual searches at about 3.5 minutes each correspond to roughly
11,000 hours. The stated floor produces 4,375 hours; the page does not bridge the gap. Angle:
the bot may work, but the customer-story headline is not reproducible from its published inputs.
Source: https://www.microsoft.com/en/customers/story/24385-worten-azure-openai

## W1. The support copilot reduced requests for a manager

A peer-reviewed field deployment followed 5,172 support agents. Resolved chats per hour rose
15%, and customer requests to speak to a manager fell almost 25% from a roughly 6% baseline,
especially for less experienced agents. The study measured requests, not completed escalations.
Source: https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044

## W2. Answer boxes trade searching for checking

A randomized Microsoft study found LLM search about twice as fast on routine comparison tasks,
but users often missed wrong answers. IBM separately reports only 28% current accuracy for its
support assistant while counting ten minutes saved when an answer is correct. Angle: direct
answers move some work from finding information to verifying it. Sources:
https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3714082 and https://www.ibm.com/case-studies/ibm-software-support

## V1. LegalZoom's adoption number beats its hours claim

LegalZoom's old intranet had less than 10% adoption. Glean says its replacement reached 70% to
90% monthly use and powered a Slack support agent with thousands of runs. The two-to-four-hours
saved claim has no disclosed method. Angle: repeated employee use is the cleaner evidence that
the search replacement solved a real problem. Source: https://www.glean.com/resources/customer-stories/legalzoom

## V2. pladis published the denominator

Seventy employees entered an eight-week Copilot program. Microsoft's fresh September 10 case
study bases the 580-hours-per-month headline on the 48 people who completed every class and the
tracker. Angle: the disclosed denominator makes the claim more useful while revealing selection
and self-reporting limits. Source: https://www.microsoft.com/en/customers/story/27237-pladis-global-microsoft-365-copilot

## C1. Enterprise RAG systems struggle to refuse

A new benchmark built 983 enterprise-style cases from production RAG queries. The best tested
model correctly refused only 42.7% of missing-knowledge cases and recognized document conflicts
44.3% of the time. Angle: Farmers' 60% call-deflection claim is incomplete without correctness,
refusal, and escalation measures. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11584

## O1. Hitachi put the assistant where employees worked

Hitachi Energy piloted for three months, replaced four employee portals, added 17 languages,
and put its assistant in Microsoft Teams for a 50,000-person rollout. ServiceNow claims a 25%
fall in service-desk requests in week one without publishing the denominator. Angle: adoption
came with consolidation, placement, training, and maintained knowledge. Source:
https://www.servicenow.com/customers/hitachi-energy-ai.html

## H1. Farmers' support bot has a 2003 twin

In 2003 LPL Financial put natural-language search in front of portal content for 4,300 agents
across 2,100 branches. Its goal was to deflect service-center and supervisor calls; it tuned
company language and fed failed queries into the content library. Angle: the operating model
behind the supposedly new AI use case is 23 years old. Source:
https://www.computerworld.com/article/1339548/enterprises-widen-the-search-net.html

## H2. Xerox's technicians became the expert system

Xerox first built software to diagnose copier faults, then learned the manuals already covered
known faults. Eureka became a peer-vetted library for unusual fixes where technicians got
authorship credit. Its builders claim more than $100 million in savings without publishing the
calculation. Angle: the durable system was the contribution and review process around the
software. Source: https://www.sri.com/publication/fcd-publications/communal-knowledge-sharing-the-eureka-story/
