## VERBATIM EXTRACT (Carscoops, via WebFetch)
Publication Date: June 11, 2026
Key Dollar Figures:
- AI offer: $27,162.79
- Dealership's actual valuation: ~$20,000
- Discrepancy: $7,000+

Summary of Events:
"Zack Giacomelli, a funeral director, attempted to sell his 2021 BMW X3 back to BMW Toronto through their online inquiry system. He engaged in text conversations with 'Quinn,' believing he was communicating with a dealership employee. The AI chatbot offered $27,162.79 — exactly matching his loan balance — and scheduled a 3:30 PM appointment.
A dealership representative later called to inform Giacomelli the offer was invalid because Quinn was an artificial intelligence system, not a person. The customer was shocked, stating: 'I was devastated. If they're going to be replacing their employees' jobs with AI, then they need to be honoring what that AI says.'
Critical Issue: The chatbot never disclosed its artificial nature during the conversation, leaving Giacomelli unaware he was negotiating with automation.
Resolution: Following media coverage by CBC News, BMW Toronto reversed course and honored the original $27,162.79 offer. The dealership acknowledged a communication breakdown occurred when the AI misinterpreted the loan amount as the purchase price. Management pledged to restrict buyback offers to human employees going forward."

## Additional detail from search snippets
- Dealership pre-owned sales manager: Scott Shadbolt. Attributed the error to "a miscommunication from a human employee causing the chatbot to interpret Giacomelli's loan balance as the proposed purchase price."
- Shadbolt told CBC the dealership wanted to "do right" by Giacomelli.
- Dealership response: restrict all future buyback offers to human employees.
- Coverage explicitly links to Moffatt v. Air Canada (2024): "In a 2024 case, Air Canada was forced to honour a fare rebate after its chatbot provided a passenger with incorrect advice about bereavement fares, with the tribunal disagreeing with Air Canada's claim that the chatbot was a separate legal entity."

## WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE LANE
- Named, established, non-tech-native org (BMW Toronto dealership, BMW Group retail network).
- The AI took an action with direct MONEY consequence: a priced buyback offer + a booked appointment.
- Blameless victim: customer had no way to know he was talking to a machine.
- The bot never disclosed it was AI — directly implicates EU AI Act Article 50 (in force 2 Aug 2026), which requires chatbots/voice agents to tell people they are dealing with a machine, and binds the DEPLOYER.
- Aftermath: offer honored only after CBC News involvement — i.e. media pressure, not policy, was the control.
- NOT first-of-kind (Moffatt v. Air Canada 2024 is), but it is the fresh 2026 instance of the archetype — and notably resolved WITHOUT a tribunal, because the company folded pre-litigation.
