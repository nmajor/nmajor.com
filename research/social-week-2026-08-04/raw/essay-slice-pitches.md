## Slice 1 — The scoreboard was the bill

**Hook:** Uber ranked engineers on a leaderboard by how much they used AI tools. On metered pricing, that scoreboard was the bill.

**Story.** Uber put Claude Code in front of ~5,000 engineers in December and burned the
entire 2026 budget by April. The internal leaderboards ranked engineers by usage; on
consumption pricing, usage = tokens = spend, so they ranked people by cost and read it as
progress. Meanwhile the COO said on a podcast that the link from all that spend to
anything a customer notices "is not there yet." Every number Uber could produce was an
input. This is the essay's core story, told straight, issue-day post.

**Sources:** the essay itself + research/uber-ai-budget/report.md (Forbes 2026-05-17,
Fortune 2026-05-26, simonwillison.net 2026-06-03).

**Why now:** the essay publishes today, 2026-08-04.

**First-person angle:** Nick's consulting line: make whoever owns the adoption target own
the budget line, and pick the output you'll judge by before the meter starts.

**Breakout:** medium-high — the story is known, but "the scoreboard was the bill" is a
compression nobody else has used.

## Slice 2 — I ran four agents. I run two now.

**Hook:** I used to run four AI agents in parallel. I cut back to two, and got more done.

**Story.** Nick's firsthand miniature of the essay's input/output trap: four parallel
agents looked like throughput, but they all finish, and then you're context-switching
across four features trying to review each honestly. He'd optimized the part that was
already cheap and built a to-do list out of the part that wasn't. Two is what he can
actually check; the real productivity move was making the agent verify its own work
end-to-end with Playwright — attacking the review step instead of flooding it.

**Sources:** first-person; consistent with the essay body.

**Why now:** the essay publishes today; this is the personal-story cut.

**First-person angle:** entirely first person — the strongest authenticity slot in the
batch.

**Breakout:** medium — first-person craft posts save well in-network; less stranger-reshare.

if both survive, they must not both run in the same week as separate posts — pick one
carrier for this anecdote.
