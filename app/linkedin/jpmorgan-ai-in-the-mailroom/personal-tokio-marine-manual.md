Tokio Marine HCC gave its AI agents the whole underwriting manual. Then it cut the reading list.

In an Automation Today interview, executive Tamer Assaad describes a submissions-prioritization system being tested before production. The team initially supplied the entire manual. Narrowing it to relevant sections improved the specificity of the agents' work.

That's a useful failure to catch during development. More material gave the system more to work through, without giving it a better answer to the particular underwriting question.

The agents organize and prioritize submissions. Underwriters still decide whether to accept the business, and the system records reasons for its scores. The underwriters also participated in development.

There isn't a measured production return to quote yet. The company plans to evaluate speed and conversion after deployment.

The work already described is specific: selecting the relevant guidance, testing it with the people making the decisions, and keeping the reasons visible to them.
