## Instagram carousel: slide-by-slide copy

NOT A SCHEDULABLE POST. Slide copy for instagram-carousel.md (.txt on purpose).

**Framework:** D, Rant Callout (external skill, carousel-frameworks.md)
**Why this framework:** the content is a strong opinion about a common practice
(forwarding vendor-adjacent case studies as neutral evidence). Conviction is the
content; the structure keeps the heat fair.
**Slides:** 7 (framework range 4-8)
**Format:** 1080x1350 (4:5). One visual template on every interior slide. Slide 1
doubles as the grid thumbnail and must stop the scroll on its own.

---

### Slide 1: Provocative claim (the thumbnail)
**Headline:** The best AI failure story I could find was an ad.
**Sub:** And I'd been quoting it.

---

### Slide 2: Escalate: what the piece is
The most-quoted cautionary tale about building your own tools:

a custom support-triage tool that replaced a $490-a-month subscription,
whose real six-month cost came out higher.

It's the only piece anyone points to that puts a number on the part everybody skips.

---

### Slide 3: Escalate: the footer
Then I read the footer.

Every byline on the site belongs to staff at a low-code and AI development agency.

The footer on every page advertises that agency.

The About page says the site has "no vendor deals."

---

### Slide 4: Escalate: the post that isn't there
It opens: "Six months ago, we documented what looked like a clean win."

There is no such post.

The only earlier piece on that site about the same product quotes completely
different numbers, and its URL now returns a 404.

---

### Slide 5: Escalate: the pattern
It sits inside a series.

One post for replacing Mixpanel. One for Klaviyo. One for BambooHR.

A sibling post argues your build method decides whether the replacement
"holds for years or breaks in 6 months."

---

### Slide 6: Fairness pivot
Don't get me wrong.

The numbers in it may well be real, and one story I checked cuts against my whole
argument: a company that dropped Salesforce for a CRM built on Lovable in 2025 is
still running it nine months on, and says the maintenance worry is overblown.

I've got nothing against agencies. What I object to is a sales asset circulating
as the neutral warning.

---

### Slide 7: Close
Read the footer before you quote the number.

Nicholas Major

**CTA (one only):** Save this before your next build-vs-buy meeting.

---

### Production checklist
- [ ] Slide 1 works alone in the feed with no carousel behind it
- [ ] One idea per slide
- [ ] Same layout, type scale and palette on slides 2-6
- [ ] No invented stats; every number on a slide is in the essay
- [ ] Legible at thumbnail size (min ~28pt at 1080 wide)
- [ ] Slide 7 has one CTA, not three
- [ ] Caption is its own hook, not a repeat of slide 1
- [ ] Do not put the agency's or the site's name on a slide. The essay names both
      in context; a square with a company name and the word "ad" on it travels
      without the context that makes the claim fair.
