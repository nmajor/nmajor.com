## Extracted

The German Act Implementing the European Artificial Intelligence Act (Gesetz zur Durchfuehrung der europaeischen Verordnung ueber Kuenstliche Intelligenz) was published in the Federal Law Gazette on 28 July 2026 and has been in force since 29 July 2026. Part of that act is the AI Market Surveillance and Innovation Promotion Act (KI-MIG), which defines when BaFin or the Federal Network Agency (BNetzA) is responsible for AI market surveillance.

BaFin obtained fining powers on 29 July 2026 and will begin sampling AI systems at banks and insurers, categorising AI as an ICT risk rather than an innovation matter.

Reported fine ceiling (secondary): up to ~$40 million for misusing AI in credit scoring, insurance pricing, and customer chatbots.

Timeline:
- 2 August 2026: transparency obligations for certain AI systems enter into force. Systems placed on the market before that date get a transitional period until 2 December 2026.
- 2 December 2027: most high-risk provisions (creditworthiness evaluation / credit scoring) come into force.

Supervision approach: BaFin will review a SAMPLE of AI applications used across many institutions in high-relevance areas; it will not examine every AI system at every bank. BaFin's Obermoeller: the regulation mandates monitoring, not comprehensive supervision.

Named institution in coverage: ING (Marina Marusenko, risk manager) quoted in the resultsense piece.

Confidence note: the ~$40M ceiling appears only in secondary coverage (techtimes/resultsense); BaFin's own release and Deloitte Legal should be checked before quoting a number.
