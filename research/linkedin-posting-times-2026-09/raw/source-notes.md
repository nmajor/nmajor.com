# Source notes

Captured 2026-09-29. These notes accompany the unedited HTML and PDF files in this directory. Times below are reported exactly as characterized by the source unless the note explicitly converts them.

## LinkedIn, 2024

- URL: https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/linkedin-ads/whats-the-best-time-to-post-on-linkedin
- File: `linkedin-2024-best-time-guidance.html`
- First-party editorial guidance, not a first-party timing experiment.
- LinkedIn summarizes three outside studies and emphasizes their disagreements. It reports broad weekday/workday agreement, but the cited studies differ on day and hour.
- Its operational recommendation is to experiment and use account analytics. It also says substance and audience relevance matter more than timing.
- The article notes that remote and flexible work weaken the old assumption that all professionals follow one 9-to-5 rhythm.

## LinkedIn, 2017

- URL: https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/content-marketing/why-i-ll-never-tell-you-when-to-post-on-linkedin
- File: `linkedin-2017-no-universal-time.html`
- First-party guidance. Old enough that its hour-level behavior should not be treated as current evidence.
- The durable point is that no universal best time exists. LinkedIn advises locating the audience by time zone and testing throughout the day.
- The article says LinkedIn's in-house analytics at the time showed activity increases before work, around lunch, and in early evening.

## Buffer, 2026 LinkedIn analysis

- URL: https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/
- File: `buffer-2026-linkedin-4.8m-posts.html`
- Published 2026-09-09.
- Buffer says it analyzed more than 4.8 million LinkedIn posts sent through Buffer. The outcome is engagement, not pure reach or qualified-lead generation.
- Times are normalized to the target audience's local time.
- Reported general window: 3 p.m. to 8 p.m. on weekdays.
- Strongest overall slot: Wednesday 4 p.m. local. Next: Friday 3 p.m. and 4 p.m.
- Day-specific top three local times: Monday 10 p.m., 5 p.m., 9 p.m.; Tuesday 4 p.m., 5 p.m., 10 p.m.; Wednesday 4 p.m., 3 p.m., 5 p.m.; Thursday 5 p.m., 7 p.m., 9 p.m.; Friday 3 p.m., 4 p.m., 5 p.m.; Saturday 9 a.m., 6 p.m., 10 p.m.; Sunday 10 p.m., 9 p.m., 6 a.m.
- Day order: Wednesday, then Thursday and Friday. Monday and Tuesday were weaker. Weekends were weaker than weekdays.
- Buffer explicitly says these are starting points and that timing cannot rescue irrelevant content.
- Limitation: this is an observational sample of posts sent through one scheduling product. The visible methodology does not isolate leadership audiences, organic personal profiles, or causal timing effects.

## Buffer, 2026 cross-platform state report

- URL: https://buffer.com/resources/state-of-social-media-engagement-2026/
- File: `buffer-2026-state-52m-posts.html`
- The report covers more than 52 million posts across platforms and describes timing as an amplifier rather than the main performance driver.
- Its LinkedIn summary matches the dedicated study: 3 p.m. to 8 p.m. weekdays, peaking Wednesday at 4 p.m.
- It says the difference between top time blocks is often smaller than people expect and cautions against causal interpretation.

## Hootsuite, 2025

- URL: https://blog.hootsuite.com/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/
- File: `hootsuite-2025-linkedin-1m-posts.html`
- Published 2025-11-19. Hootsuite says it partnered with Critical Truth to analyze more than 1 million social posts. The article does not clearly disclose the LinkedIn-only post count in the visible methodology.
- Data came from 118 countries and times were localized. The heatmaps refer to Q4 2024.
- Summary recommendation: Tuesday and Wednesday mornings. The page contains an internal inconsistency, describing the overall peak as both 8-9 a.m. and 4-6 a.m.
- Day-specific local times: Monday 11 a.m.; Tuesday 6-8 a.m.; Wednesday 9 a.m.; Thursday 2 p.m.; Friday 8 p.m.; Saturday 4-5 a.m.; Sunday 6 a.m.
- Professional-services result: Thursday 8-10 a.m. and Saturday 10 a.m.-1 p.m.
- Technology result: Monday 11 a.m.
- Limitation: the article's contradictory overall windows and incomplete LinkedIn sample disclosure make it a secondary prior, not a schedule authority.

## Metricool, 2026 LinkedIn study

- URL: https://metricool.com/wp-content/uploads/Linkedin-Study-2026-EN.pdf
- File: `metricool-2026-linkedin-study.pdf`
- Metricool analyzed 673,658 posts from 63,108 global LinkedIn accounts during January-February 2025 and January-February 2026. It includes Company Pages and Personal Profiles.
- The timing section says 9 a.m.-12 p.m. has the highest publishing volume. That measures when accounts publish, not which hour earns the most engagement, so it cannot establish an optimal posting time.
- The study says nearly 40% of Personal Profile interactions happen on day one, and that most impressions and interactions arrive in the first two days.
- It excludes accounts with no posts and posts with zero impressions or zero interactions. That selection matters when interpreting averages.

## Sprout Social posting resources

- URL: https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-posting-resources/
- File: `sprout-social-posting-resources.html`
- Sprout describes its Best Times series as analyzing billions of engagements across thousands of social profiles. The captured overview does not disclose the LinkedIn-specific sample or exact day-hour table.
- Useful only as corroboration that account-specific timing systems should learn from the account's own interaction history.
