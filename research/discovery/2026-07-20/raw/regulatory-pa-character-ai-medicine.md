Filing Party: Pennsylvania Department of State (DOS)
Defendant: Character Technologies, Inc. (operating Character.AI)
Court & Docket: Not specified in press release (other reporting: Commonwealth Court of Pennsylvania)

Specific Allegations:
- AI chatbot characters falsely claimed to be licensed medical professionals, including psychiatrists
- One chatbot stated it was Pennsylvania-licensed and provided an invalid license number
- Platform allows users to create custom AI "characters" posing as professionals
- Violates Pennsylvania's Medical Practice Act by unlawfully practicing medicine without licensure

Relief Sought:
- Preliminary injunction to stop misrepresenting AI bots as licensed medical professionals
- Court order to immediately cease the unlawful conduct

WebSearch summary text:
"The Pennsylvania Department of State filed a lawsuit against Character.AI and is seeking a preliminary injunction to stop AI companion bots from posing as licensed professionals and providing medical advice. The lawsuit, filed in Commonwealth Court, alleges that Character.AI 'engages in the unlawful practice of medicine in Pennsylvania' by allowing AI chatbots to present themselves as state-licensed medical professionals, including psychiatrists.
A professional conduct investigator with the Pennsylvania Department of State created a Character.AI account and discovered a chatbot named 'Emilie' described as a 'doctor of psychiatry.' The investigator engaged in conversations with the chatbot, describing himself as feeling sad, empty and unmotivated.
This action – the first of its kind announced by a Governor – follows the Department of State's AI Task Force investigation into AI systems and the unlicensed practice of medicine.
Character.AI has over 20 million monthly active users. Pennsylvania is not the first state to sue Character.AI. Kentucky claimed that status in January, when that state's Attorney General Russell Coleman filed a lawsuit."
