# US federal agencies — high-impact AI use cases paused, retired, or rolled back at the OMB compliance deadline

FETCHED: 2026-07-20
SOURCE TYPE: Secondary reporting (FedScoop) built on **primary artifacts**: the agencies' own published AI use-case inventories plus on-record spokesperson statements. FedScoop contacted 28 agencies. The inventories themselves are public primary documents and can be diffed version-to-version.

SOURCE: https://fedscoop.com/federal-agencies-ai-inventory-risk-management-deadline
(FedScoop, Lindsey Wilkinson, 9 April 2026 — "AI risk management deadline hits federal agencies. Not all were ready.")

## Context
OMB required federal agencies to implement risk-management practices for **high-impact AI use cases by 3 April 2026 — or terminate them**. The deadline forced agencies to publicly declare which deployed AI they were killing. This is a rare structural forcing function that makes abandonment visible.

## Verbatim

> The deadline for federal agencies to implement risk management practices for high-impact AI use cases — or terminate them — has come and gone, but a handful of departments are still working to complete their requirements.

> FedScoop reached out to 28 federal agencies to inquire about the steps they have taken to ensure compliance within the April 3 timeframe. Some agencies fulfilled the requirements, others reclassified use cases or still have a couple boxes to check. A few appear to have missed the deadline entirely.

**Department of Veterans Affairs — the concrete rollback:**
> Similarly, the Department of Veterans Affairs told FedScoop that it has complied with OMB's requirements. The agency quietly uploaded an updated version of its AI inventory that featured new sections for the risk management practices. The VA filled in the nine compliance-related fields for its 90 deployed high-impact use cases, which include the technology assisting in reviews of results from colonoscopy procedures and breast exams. **Four high-impact use cases were rolled back from deployed to pre-deployment or retired, making them exempt from the compliance requirements.**

**Department of Labor:**
> "At this time, DOL has no active non‑compliant high‑risk AI use cases," a spokesperson told FedScoop. **"Any use case that did not meet federal standards has been paused or discontinued."**
> While the agency did not identify risk levels of use cases in its prior inventory, a newly published version categorizes just one use case as high-impact: an AI-powered scrubber of personally identifiable information.

**Department of State:**
> While a Department of State spokesperson said none of its AI use cases were decommissioned, **one of its high-impact use cases in the inventory uploaded this month has been retired after appearing in a pilot phase in the inventory post earlier this year.** The agency's other two high-impact use cases had fulfilled some of the risk management requirements, but neither use case had information about an established appeal process in the updated inventory.

**General Services Administration:**
> The General Services Administration did not post an AI inventory when other agencies did earlier this year but has since published one. A GSA spokesperson said it has thus far not had to terminate any AI use case for non-compliance with its requirements, although its inventory does not categorize use cases by risk level.

## Real numbers
- VA: **90 deployed high-impact AI use cases**; **4 rolled back** from deployed to pre-deployment or retired.
- State Dept: **1 high-impact use case retired** after a pilot phase — while the department simultaneously told FedScoop nothing was decommissioned (note the contradiction between the spokesperson's statement and the department's own published inventory).
- DOL: unspecified number "paused or discontinued"; inventory now lists only 1 high-impact use case.
- 28 agencies contacted by FedScoop.

## Why this is worth keeping
- It is one of the very few places where **AI abandonment is a matter of public record with a named deploying organisation and a documented before/after**, because a regulatory deadline forced disclosure. Everywhere else, abandonment is invisible.
- The State Department detail is the sharpest item: the official line was "nothing was decommissioned," and the department's own published inventory says otherwise. That gap — between what organisations *say* about their AI and what their records show — is the reusable insight.
- The VA rollback mechanism is also notable: reclassifying "deployed" back to "pre-deployment" makes the use case **exempt from compliance requirements**. Whether that is genuine caution or definitional dodge is an open question worth flagging rather than asserting.

## Caveats
- No dollar figures anywhere. No named vendor. No description of *what* the retired use cases actually did.
- "Rolled back to pre-deployment or retired" bundles two very different things; FedScoop does not break out which was which.
- Federal agencies are not mid-market companies. Value here is the *mechanism and the paper trail*, not the org profile.
