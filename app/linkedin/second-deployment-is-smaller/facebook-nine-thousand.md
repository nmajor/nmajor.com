Johnson & Johnson got to nearly 9,000 separate AI projects before anyone checked which ones were actually working.

Their CIO calls the original approach "a thousand flowers". When they finally measured it, somewhere between 10 and 15% of those projects were producing about 80% of the value.

So they cut it down to four: a helper for sales reps, drug discovery, supply chain risk, and an internal HR chatbot that handles roughly 10 million employee questions a year.

Nine thousand is the number worth staring at. That's a pile nobody was sorting through.

And you can only find the useful 10% by running things long enough to measure them, so the sorting is the actual skill, not the starting.

If your company is counting how many AI projects it has, the more interesting question is how many got switched off this year. A team that can answer that is measuring. A team that can't is collecting.
